# Two counters make ten

Grade 1 · Properties, missing addends and equality

## From eight and six to ten and four

**[1.OA.B.3](https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/Content/1/OA/B/3/) · [1.OA.C.6](https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/Content/1/OA/C/6/).** There are eight counters in a ten-frame and six beside it. How many altogether? Each letter stands for one counter. The two marked *b* belong to the group of six. A dot is an empty space.

```text
          frame        beside it
Before    a a a a a    b b c c c c
          a a a · ·

After     a a a a a    c c c c
          a a a b b
```

Move the two *b* counters into the empty spaces. Six splits into two and four. The **addends** are the amounts joined. Regrouping them gives **8 + 6 = 8 + (2 + 4) = (8 + 2) + 4 = 10 + 4 = 14**. Counting on from eight through 9, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14 checks the six added counters.

**Parent bridge.** The two counters that fill the frame came from the six beside it. All fourteen are still on the mat, so 10 + 4 describes the same collection as 8 + 6.

Read the original mat from the other end. Six beside the frame and eight inside give **6 + 8 = 14** too. Addition permits exchanging the addends (*commutativity*) and changing their grouping (*associativity*). Subtraction keeps its order. With eight counters, taking away six leaves two. Starting with six, there are too few to take away eight. Reversing a subtraction changes the question.

## Counting moves and finding a missing part

**[1.OA.C.5](https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/Content/1/OA/C/5/) · [1.OA.B.4](https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/Content/1/OA/B/4/) · [1.OA.C.6](https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/Content/1/OA/C/6/).** Begin with eight. How many steps reach thirteen? Each arrow is **one move**. The starting number is already on the path.

```text
Start 8 → 9 → 10 → 11 → 12 → 13     five moves forward
Start 13 → 12 → 11 → 10 → 9 → 8     five moves back
```

So 8 + 5 = 13 and 13 − 5 = 8. Now ask **13 − 8 = □**. Think of the eight as a known part of thirteen. The missing addend in **8 + □ = 13** is five, as the five forward moves show. Replacing the box gives 8 + 5 = 13 and **13 − 8 = 5**. Check by removing all eight in two steps. Take three to reach ten, then take the remaining five. Since 3 + 5 = 8, the calculation is 13 − 8 = (13 − 3) − 5 = 10 − 5 = 5.

## Facts within ten

**[1.OA.C.6](https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/Content/1/OA/C/6/).** A known sum can supply another fact without counting every object again.

| Fact to find | Reasoning | Check |
|---|---|---|
| 4 + 4 | Two pairs and two more pairs give 2 + 2 + 2 + 2 = 8. | Removing four from eight leaves four. |
| 4 + 5 | Five is one more than four, so this is one more than 4 + 4. The sum is 9. | 9 − 5 = 4. |
| 10 − 7 | Seven needs three to fill a ten-frame. The missing part is 3. | 7 + 3 = 10. |
| 6 − 6 | Taking all six leaves 0. | 0 + 6 = 6. |
| 6 + 0 | An empty second group adds no counters. The sum is 6. | The first six still account for every counter. |

At the top of the within-20 range, 9 + 10 can be seen as 10 + 9 = 19. Adding one more to the nine gives 10 + 10 = 20. These strategies support fluency within ten. Judging a child's fluency requires observing the child use facts.

## What the equals sign says

**[1.OA.D.7](https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/Content/1/OA/D/7/).** Each side of an equation names a quantity. Compare their values to decide whether the equation is true.

| Proposed equation | Left side | Right side | Decision |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| 8 + 6 = 16 − 2 | 14 | 14 | True. Both sides name fourteen. |
| 13 − 8 = 2 + 4 | 5 | 6 | False. The right side is one larger. |
| 7 = 10 − 3 | 7 | 7 | True. The single number can be on the left. |

## Which number belongs in the box?

**[1.OA.D.8](https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/Content/1/OA/D/8/).** Read the role of the box before choosing a calculation.

| Equation | Find the missing quantity | Replace the box in the original |
|---|---|---|
| □ + 3 = 11 | The first part is 11 − 3 = 8. | 8 + 3 = 11 |
| 8 + □ = 13 | From eight, two reach ten and three more reach thirteen. The second part is 2 + 3 = 5. | 8 + 5 = 13 |
| 6 + 4 = □ | Six and its four empty frame spaces make ten. | 6 + 4 = 10 |
| □ − 4 = 7 | Put back the four removed. The start was 7 + 4 = 11. | 11 − 4 = 7 |
| 11 − □ = 7 | Count back 10, 9, 8, 7. Four were removed. | 11 − 4 = 7 |
| 13 − 8 = □ | The missing part joins eight to make thirteen. | 13 − 8 = 5 |
