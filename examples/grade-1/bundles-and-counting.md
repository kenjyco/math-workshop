# How many sticks are in the bundles?

Grade 1 · Counting through 120 and reading tens and ones

Count and write through 120. Compare two-digit numerals.

## One tie around ten sticks

**[1.NBT.B.2.a](https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/Content/1/NBT/B/2/a/).** Count the sticks in two rows of five. Slide them together and put a band around all ten. In these drawings, a bracket encloses one banded bundle.

```text
Loose          Banded
I I I I I
I I I I I      [IIIIIIIIII]
```

How many sticks can you lift by picking up the one bundle? **Ten.** Undo the band and lay them back in two rows. Five and five still give ten. In the [Kindergarten frame](../kindergarten/tens-and-teens.md#how-many-beyond-a-full-frame), the ten were individual ones. The bundle lets us count that same quantity as **one ten**. Below, `T` means one such bundle and `i` means one loose stick.

## Reading both digits of 42

**[1.NBT.B.2](https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/Content/1/NBT/B/2/).** A tray holds `T T T T   i i`. How should we label it? Count the bundles by tens to 10, 20, 30, 40. Touch the loose ones to count 41, 42. Write **42**, read *forty-two*. The left digit counts four tens and the right digit counts two ones. To check, rebuild from the label. Four piles of ten and a pair give 10 + 10 + 10 + 10 + 2 = 42. Untying one bundle gives three tens and twelve loose sticks, still 30 + 12 = 42.

## Keeping one ten in every teen

**[1.NBT.B.2.b](https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/Content/1/NBT/B/2/b/).** The same tied bundle stays on the tray. How does the numeral change as loose sticks are added?

| Bundle | Loose sticks | Numeral |
|:---:|:---|---:|
| T | i | 11 |
| T | ii | 12 |
| T | iii | 13 |
| T | iiii | 14 |
| T | iiiii | 15 |
| T | iiiiii | 16 |
| T | iiiiiii | 17 |
| T | iiiiiiii | 18 |
| T | iiiiiiiii | 19 |

Compare **14** with **17**. Match their two bundles to each other and match four loose sticks from each tray. Seventeen has three extras. Check by adding those to fourteen in order, 15, 16, 17. Across the table the ones digit goes up by one each time because the ten stays tied.

## Exact tens and an empty ones place

**[1.NBT.B.2.c](https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/Content/1/NBT/B/2/c/).** Each row adds a bundle and leaves the loose-stick space empty. Read across to label the tray.

| Bundles on tray | Tens | Loose ones | Numeral |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| T | 1 | 0 | 10 |
| T T | 2 | 0 | 20 |
| T T T | 3 | 0 | 30 |
| T T T T | 4 | 0 | 40 |
| T T T T T | 5 | 0 | 50 |
| T T T T T T | 6 | 0 | 60 |
| T T T T T T T | 7 | 0 | 70 |
| T T T T T T T T | 8 | 0 | 80 |
| T T T T T T T T T | 9 | 0 | 90 |

To build **60**, take six bundles and leave the loose-stick space empty. Check the six by making two groups of three bundles. Each group counts to thirty, and 30 + 30 = 60.

**Parent bridge.** In 60 the 6 counts bundles worth ten each. The 0 records the empty ones place. Writing just 6 would describe six single sticks.

## Comparing two trays

**[1.NBT.B.3](https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/Content/1/NBT/B/3/).** Match tens first. With equal tens, match loose sticks. Read each comparison from left to right.

| Trays | Comparison aloud | Rebuild and check |
|---|---|---|
| `TTTT ii` and `TTTT iiiii` | **42 < 45**, forty-two is less than forty-five. | Both have 40. Two loose sticks match two of the five, leaving three on the right. |
| `TTTT` and `TTT iiiiiiiii` | **40 > 39**, forty is greater than thirty-nine. | Match three tens. Untie the fourth ten. Its ten sticks exceed the nine loose sticks by one. |
| `TT iii` and `TT iii` | **23 = 23**, twenty-three equals twenty-three. | Each tray rebuilds as 10 + 10 + 3. Every bundle and loose stick has a partner. |

## Counting from 97 to 120

**[1.NBT.A.1](https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/Content/1/NBT/A/1/).** A box already holds **97** sticks. Start at that number and add one stick at each step until the box holds 120.

```text
97, 98, 99, 100
101, 102, 103, 104, 105, 106, 107, 108, 109, 110
111, 112, 113, 114, 115, 116, 117, 118, 119, 120
```

At ninety-nine there are nine bundles and nine loose sticks. Add one more loose stick and tie the new ten. Count the ten bundles to **one hundred**, written **100**. From any supplied start below 120, adding one gives the next number until 120 is reached.

Which numeral belongs on this box?

```text
T T T T T    T T T T T    T    i i i i
```

The first two groups each hold fifty sticks. The last bundle and four loose sticks give fourteen more. Count 100, 110, then 111, 112, 113, 114. Write **114**, read *one hundred fourteen*. Check the other direction from the spoken number. Lay out one hundred sticks in ten bundles, then one more bundle and four sticks. That rebuilds the pictured box. At 119 the eleventh bundle has nine loose sticks beside it. One more completes a twelfth bundle. Twelve bundles count to **120**, *one hundred twenty*.
