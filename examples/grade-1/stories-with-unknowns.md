# What do we need to find?

Grade 1 · Quantities in additive stories

Each amount and total is within 20. [Additive situations, Table 1, p. 88](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=88)

## Buttons going into or coming out of a tin

**[1.OA.A.1](https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/Content/1/OA/A/1/).** Each story starts afresh. Read the question first and name the amount it asks for. Then uncover the equation at the right. Each dot in these little drawings stands for one button.

| Story | What does the box count? | Equation, work and check |
|---|---|---|
| There were six buttons in a tin. Jo dropped in three. How many are inside now? | The **result**, after adding. | 6 + 3 = □. Start `●●●●●●`, add `●●●`. Count on 7, 8, 9. **9 buttons are inside.** Taking three out returns six. |
| There were six in the tin. Jo added some. Now there are nine. How many went in? | The **change**, the buttons added. | 6 + □ = 9. Set aside the original six from nine. The other three went in. **3 buttons were added.** 6 + 3 = 9. |
| Jo added three buttons and found nine in the tin. How many were there to begin with? | The **start**, before adding. | □ + 3 = 9. Lift out the three new buttons. Nine less three leaves six. **6 buttons were there at the start.** 6 + 3 = 9. |
| There were nine buttons. Jo took three out. How many stayed in the tin? | The **result**, after removing. | 9 − 3 = □. The nine split into out `●●●` and in `●●●●●●`. **6 buttons stayed.** Putting three back gives nine. |
| There were nine. Jo took some out, leaving six. How many came out? | The **change**, the buttons removed. | 9 − □ = 6. Match six of the nine to the six that stayed. Three are unmatched. **3 buttons came out.** 9 − 3 = 6. |
| Jo took three out and six stayed in the tin. How many were in it before? | The **start**, before removing. | □ − 3 = 6. Put the three beside the six that stayed. 6 + 3 = 9. **9 buttons were in it before.** 9 − 3 = 6. |

## Two parts on a tray

**[1.OA.A.1](https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/Content/1/OA/A/1/).** The tray holds only round and square buttons. These are **parts** of one collection. They need not arrive or leave.

| Story | Requested quantity | Model, answer and check |
|---|---|---|
| Four are round and three are square. How many buttons are on the tray? | The whole tray. | `○○○○  ■■■` gives 4 + 3 = □. Count on 5, 6, 7. **7 buttons** in all. Removing either group leaves the other. |
| Seven are on the tray. Three are square. How many are round? | The round part. | □ + 3 = 7. Set aside `■■■` from seven. Four are left. **4 round buttons**. 4 + 3 = 7. |
| Seven are on the tray. Four are round. How many are square? | The square part. | 4 + □ = 7. From four, count 5, 6, 7. **3 square buttons**. 4 + 3 = 7. |

**Both parts unknown.** A different tray holds four buttons. How many could be round and how many square? We need **two counts**, one for each shape. In **4 = □ + △**, the box counts round buttons and the triangle stands for the number of square buttons. Begin with all four square. Replace one square with one round each time.

| Round | Square | Rebuild the whole |
|---:|---:|---|
| 0 | 4 | 4 = 0 + 4 |
| 1 | 3 | 4 = 1 + 3 |
| 2 | 2 | 4 = 2 + 2 |
| 3 | 1 | 4 = 3 + 1 |
| 4 | 0 | 4 = 4 + 0 |

Every row accounts for four buttons. The round count takes each possible value from zero through four, so these are all the ways when the parts are named.

## Two collections side by side

**[1.OA.A.1](https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/Content/1/OA/A/1/).** Pair one of May's buttons with one of Teo's. The **difference** counts the buttons left without a partner. These stories use the same two collections with different information given.

| Story | Name the unknown first | Equation, answer and check |
|---|---|---|
| May has eight and Teo has five. How many more does May have? How many fewer does Teo have? | The difference in each question. | 5 + □ = 8. Counting on 6, 7, 8 gives **3 more for May**, or **3 fewer for Teo**. 5 + 3 = 8. |
| Teo has five, three fewer than May. How many does May have? | May's larger collection. | □ − 3 = 5. Restore the three extra buttons to the five paired ones. **May has 8.** 8 − 3 = 5. |
| May has eight, three more than Teo. How many does Teo have? | Teo's smaller collection. | □ + 3 = 8. Remove the three extra from eight. **Teo has 5.** 5 + 3 = 8. |

The wording can change while the button rows stay the same. “Teo has five and May has three more than Teo” gives 5 + 3 = 8 for May. “May has eight and Teo has three fewer than May” gives 8 − 3 = 5 for Teo. In both versions the name after “how many does ___ have?” tells us which whole row to report.

```text
           paired      unmatched
May        ● ● ● ● ●   ● ● ●
Teo        ● ● ● ● ●
```

**Parent bridge.** Run a finger down each matched column. The three marks extending beyond Teo's row measure the difference, 8 − 5 = 3. That calculation answers “how many more?” In the last story, writing “3 buttons” would repeat the given difference. Counting the five matched columns answers the question about Teo's collection.

## Buttons from three pockets

**[1.OA.A.2](https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/Content/1/OA/A/2/).** Jo empties three pockets onto a mat. The first holds eight buttons, the second five, and the third seven. How many land on the mat? The unknown is the **total across all three pockets**.

```text
First    ●●●● ●●●●
Second   ●●●●●
Third    ●●●● ●●●
```

Write 8 + 5 + 7 = □. Take two from the second row to join the first eight. That leaves three in the second row. Those three and the seven in the third make another ten. **10 + 10 = 20 buttons on the mat.** Check in pocket order without moving any buttons. Eight and five make thirteen. Seven more take thirteen to twenty. Each pocket contributes once.
