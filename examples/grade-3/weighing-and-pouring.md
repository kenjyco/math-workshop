# Which unit belongs beside the reading?

Grade 3 · G38 · `units`, `model` · MP5 · [Goal and source, p. 24](../../goals/grade-3-fractions-and-measurement.md#g38-measuring-mass-and-liquid-volume-and-operating-in-one-unit)

## Three sample readings

**Mass** tells how much matter an object contains. A balance compares masses and a calibrated scale gives a mass reading. **Liquid volume** measures how much liquid there is. Use the marks on a measuring jug to read it. These are constructed readings.

| Object and benchmark | Estimate | Measurement |
|---|---|---|
| A packet of buttons feels about as heavy as a 50 g reference mass. | About 50 grams | Put the packet on one pan of a level equal-arm balance. A 50 g mass and a 5 g mass together level the other pan. The packet has mass **55 g**. |
| A sack feels close to three 1 kg bags. | About 3 kilograms | Zero a platform scale and set the sack on it. Between 2 and 4 kg its marks read 2, 3, 4 at equal distances. The pointer stops at the middle mark, **3 kg**. |
| Water fills about three 1 L bottles. | About 3 liters | Set the jug level. Read the bottom of the water surface at eye level against the marks below. |

```text
Jug reading       liters
                  4 ├
water surface  ───3 ├
                  2 ├
                  1 ├
empty level       0 ├
```

From empty to the 4 L mark there are four equal volume intervals, so each is 1 L. The water reaches the third mark above zero, giving **3 L**.

**Parent bridge.** Read the label with the number. The sack's 3 is kilograms on a mass scale. The jug's 3 is liters on a liquid scale. Writing the unit preserves what was measured.

## Four questions about measured amounts

| Question | One-step calculation and answer | Recover a given amount |
|---|---|---|
| A 55 g packet and a 35 g packet go on one pan. What is their total mass? | 55 + 35 = 50 + 30 + 5 + 5 = **90 g** | Remove 35 g from 90 g to recover 55 g. |
| A jug contains 5 L. Pour out 2 L. How much stays? | 5 − 2 = **3 L** | 3 L left and 2 L poured make 5 L. |
| Four sacks each have mass 3 kg. What is their total mass? | 4 × 3 = 3 + 3 + 3 + 3 = **12 kg** | Sharing 12 kg among four equal sacks gives back 3 kg each. |
| Share 12 L of water equally among three cans. How much goes in each? | Giving 1 L to each can uses 3 L per round. Four rounds use 12 L. 12 ÷ 3 = **4 L per can**. | Three cans holding 4 L each restore all 12 L. |
