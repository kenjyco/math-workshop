# Which end of the line is closer?

Grade 3 · Rounding and place-value arithmetic

## Both sides of a midpoint

A walk along the number line measures the distance to each neighboring multiple. At a midpoint the walks tie. For whole-number rounding we choose the upper multiple in a tie.

```text
90──91──92──93──94──95──96──97──98──99──100
200──210──220──230──240──250──260──270──280──290──300
```

On the second line each long step is ten ones. The neighbors 249 and 251 sit one unit on either side of 250.

| Round to | Start | Walk to lower end | Walk to upper end | Nearest multiple |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| ten | 90 | 0 | 10 | 90 |
| ten | 94 | 4 | 6 | 90 |
| ten | 95 | 5 | 5 | 100 |
| ten | 96 | 6 | 4 | 100 |
| hundred | 200 | 0 | 100 | 200 |
| hundred | 249 | 49 | 51 | 200 |
| hundred | 250 | 50 | 50 | 300 |
| hundred | 251 | 51 | 49 | 300 |

An exact multiple already has distance zero to itself. Rounding 96 to the nearest ten crosses into the next hundred because 100 is only four steps away.

## Two ways to count a delivery

A shop receives 368 envelopes and then 257. How many arrived? Rounding each delivery to the nearest ten gives an **estimate** of 370 + 260 = 630 envelopes.

**Exact count by partial sums.** Combine the hundreds, tens and ones separately. (300 + 200) + (60 + 50) + (8 + 7) = 500 + 110 + 15 = 610 + 15 = **625 envelopes**.

**Exact count by moving envelopes.** Move two from the second pile to the first. The piles now hold 370 and 255. Move another 30 to the first pile. The two piles now hold 400 and 225, still **625 envelopes**. Every move adds and takes away the same amount.

Check by taking back the second delivery. 625 − 200 = 425, then 425 − 50 = 375 and 375 − 7 = 368.

## Taking envelopes from a full carton

A carton of 602 envelopes supplies 178 for a mailing. Estimate 600 − 180 = 420 left. To find the exact count, take out 180 and put back the two extra. 602 − 100 = 502, then 502 − 80 = 422, then 422 + 2 = **424 envelopes left**. Adding back the mailing gives 424 + 100 = 524, 524 + 70 = 594, 594 + 8 = 602.

**Parent bridge.** The estimates 630 and 420 tell us the sizes to expect. Writing 62 for the first total would put it hundreds of envelopes away. Counting the exact piles settles the last few envelopes.
