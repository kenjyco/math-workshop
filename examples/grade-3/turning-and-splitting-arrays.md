# What stays the same when a tray turns?

Grade 3 · Multiplication properties and division facts

## Turn, bundle and split

Turn a tray a quarter turn. Its three rows of four become four rows of three. Each dot travels with the tray.

```text
● ● ● ●       ● ● ●
● ● ● ●       ● ● ●
● ● ● ●       ● ● ●
              ● ● ●
```

4 + 4 + 4 and 3 + 3 + 3 + 3 both count **12 dots**. The factors can switch places. Now pack two boxes with three sleeves in each and four counters in each sleeve. Counting sleeves first gives (2 × 3) × 4 = 6 × 4 = 24. Counting counters in one box first gives 2 × (3 × 4) = 2 × 12 = 24. Each count includes all the sleeves once.

A wider tray has seven rows of eight. Slide a divider after the fifth column.

```text
● ● ● ● ● | ● ● ●
● ● ● ● ● | ● ● ●
● ● ● ● ● | ● ● ●
● ● ● ● ● | ● ● ●
● ● ● ● ● | ● ● ●
● ● ● ● ● | ● ● ●
● ● ● ● ● | ● ● ●
```

Each row has 5 + 3 dots. Seven fives give 35 and seven threes give 21. Together 7 × (5 + 3) = 35 + 21 = **56 dots**. Counting by eights through the seven rows gives 8, 16, 24, 32, 40, 48, 56.

**Parent bridge.** Cover the three right columns. The visible part is the known fact 7 × 5. Uncovering those columns adds 7 × 3 to it. Both smaller products keep all seven rows.

## Reading back from a product

The 56-dot tray gives 56 ÷ 7 = 8 dots per row and 56 ÷ 8 = 7 rows. Each division starts with the total. Return to it with 7 × 8 = 56 or 8 × 7 = 56.

Five empty bags hold 5 × 0 = 0 buttons. Sharing zero buttons among five bags gives 0 ÷ 5 = 0 per bag. Zero bags of five also give 0 × 5 = 0. One bag of nine holds 1 × 9 = 9 buttons. All nine in one bag give 9 ÷ 1 = 9, and packing one per bag uses 9 ÷ 1 = 9 bags. Check 9 × 1 = 9. Every division here has a positive divisor.

Read back from any product cell. Dividing its value by a positive row heading recovers the column heading. Dividing by a positive column heading recovers the row heading. For instance, the zero in row 0 and column 9 gives 0 ÷ 9 = 0.

The calculation range also includes products or dividends up to 100. For instance, ten groups of ten make 100, so 100 ÷ 10 = 10. The table records what is to be recalled. Evidence that a learner recalls these facts and calculates fluently comes from their own work.
