# Which count answers the question?

Grade 3 · G31 · `model`, `equal`, `round` · MP1 and MP4 · [Goal and source, p. 23](../../goals/grade-3-operations-and-area.md#g31-representing-two-step-whole-number-problems)

## Stamps left on a sheet

A sheet holds 30 stamps. Three envelopes each need four stamps. **How many stamps remain?** Let R count the stamps remaining.

1. Three sets of four use 4 + 4 + 4 = **12 stamps used**.
2. Take those 12 off the sheet. 30 − 10 − 2 = **18 stamps left**.

The equation is R = 30 − 3 × 4. Without parentheses, multiplication comes before subtraction. Substituting the counts gives 18 = 30 − 12. Mentally, using a little more than ten should leave a little less than twenty. Returning the twelve used stamps restores the sheet.

**Parent bridge.** Ask what 12 labels on the stamp sheet. Those stamps went onto envelopes. The letter R labels the other 18 still on the sheet. Naming the first answer tells us which step is still needed.

## Seeds for four beds

Each of four beds gets 8 pea seeds and 3 bean seeds. **How many seeds go into all the beds?** Let S count seeds in all four.

1. One bed gets 8 + 3 = **11 seeds**.
2. Count beds in pairs. Two beds get 22, and another pair gets 22, giving **44 seeds altogether**.

Write S = 4 × (8 + 3). The parentheses gather every seed for one bed before that count is repeated. Substitute 44 = 4 × 11. Rounding eleven to ten gives about 40 seeds. The extra seed for each bed accounts for the other four.

## Cards for a table

Deal 36 blank cards equally to six tables. Then give one table 2 spare cards. **How many cards does that table have now?** Let C count its cards.

1. Six cards for each of six tables use all 36. The first share is **6 cards**.
2. The two spares bring that table to **8 cards**.

Write C = 36 ÷ 6 + 2. Division comes before addition here. Substitute 8 = 6 + 2. To check backward, set aside two spare cards and deal the remaining six-card share six times to recover 36.
