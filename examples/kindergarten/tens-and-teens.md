# The empty spaces and the extra ones

Kindergarten · Making ten and building teen numbers

## How many more will fill the frame?

**[K.OA.A.4](https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/Content/K/OA/A/4/).** A ten-frame has two rows of five spaces. Each ● marks one occupied space and each ○ one empty space. With seven counters already in the frame, how many more will fit?

```text
● ● ● ● ●
● ● ○ ○ ○
```

The full top row holds five and the bottom row holds two. Put one counter in each of the three empty spaces. Now there are two full rows of five. **Seven needs three more to make ten.** The missing partner is called a *complement to ten*.

| Start | Top row | Bottom row | Fill the empty spaces |
|---:|:---:|:---:|---|
| 1 | `●○○○○` | `○○○○○` | 1 + 9 = 10 |
| 2 | `●●○○○` | `○○○○○` | 2 + 8 = 10 |
| 3 | `●●●○○` | `○○○○○` | 3 + 7 = 10 |
| 4 | `●●●●○` | `○○○○○` | 4 + 6 = 10 |
| 5 | `●●●●●` | `○○○○○` | 5 + 5 = 10 |
| 6 | `●●●●●` | `●○○○○` | 6 + 4 = 10 |
| 7 | `●●●●●` | `●●○○○` | 7 + 3 = 10 |
| 8 | `●●●●●` | `●●●○○` | 8 + 2 = 10 |
| 9 | `●●●●●` | `●●●●○` | 9 + 1 = 10 |

Read down the frames. Each new counter fills one space, so the number still needed drops by one. The filled and empty spaces always account for all ten.

## How many beyond a full frame?

**[K.NBT.A.1](https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/Content/K/NBT/A/1/).** There are sixteen loose counters. Fill the top row and then the bottom row with one counter per space. Put the counters left over beside the frame.

```text
● ● ● ● ●      ● ● ●
● ● ● ● ●      ● ● ●
  full frame    extra ones
```

The frame holds ten individual ones. The two short rows beside it hold six more. Taking sixteen apart gives **16 = 10 + 6**. To build sixteen again, keep the ten visible and count on through 11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 16, touching one extra at each word.

Use the same full frame for each row below. Read from the two groups to the total to build the number. Start at the total and separate the ten counters in the frame from those beside it to take the number apart.

| Full frame (top / bottom) | Extra ones beside it | Total and its parts |
|:---:|---|---|
| `●●●●● / ●●●●●` | `●` | 11 = 10 + 1 |
| `●●●●● / ●●●●●` | `●●` | 12 = 10 + 2 |
| `●●●●● / ●●●●●` | `●●●` | 13 = 10 + 3 |
| `●●●●● / ●●●●●` | `●●●●` | 14 = 10 + 4 |
| `●●●●● / ●●●●●` | `●●●●●` | 15 = 10 + 5 |
| `●●●●● / ●●●●●` | `●●●●●●` | 16 = 10 + 6 |
| `●●●●● / ●●●●●` | `●●●●●●●` | 17 = 10 + 7 |
| `●●●●● / ●●●●●` | `●●●●●●●●` | 18 = 10 + 8 |
| `●●●●● / ●●●●●` | `●●●●●●●●●` | 19 = 10 + 9 |

Check the endpoints by moving the extras. For 11, move the single extra from the right of the frame to the left. Counting it first and then the ten in the frame still gives eleven. For 19, move the nine extras into three rows of three. Count those rows to 3, 6 and 9, then the two frame rows to 14 and 19. Every counter is still one.

**Parent bridge.** At seven, “how many more to ten?” counts the three empty spaces. At sixteen, “how many beyond ten?” counts the six counters outside a full frame. The ten inside are still ten separate ones. Treating ten ones as a single bundled ten comes in Grade 1.
