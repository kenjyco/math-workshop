# Which total answers the order?

## G49 · Follow the units to the question

[G49 · goal and source](../../goals/grade-4-whole-numbers.md#g49-choosing-operations-and-interpreting-what-remains) · 4.OA.A.3 · `model`, `multi-div`, `round` · MP2, MP4

**Shipment.** Each carton holds 4 packs of 6 clips. Five cartons arrive. Each carton is 30 cm long. How many clips arrive? Let C count shipped clips. One carton holds 4 × 6 = 24 clips. Then C = 5 × 24 = **120 clips**. A packing slip that says “24 clips per carton” is right. A shipment total of 24 would account for just one carton. Reverse the chain: 120 ÷ 5 = 24 per carton and 24 ÷ 4 = 6 per pack. The length describes the box and supplies no clip count. Rounding 24 to 20 gives about 100 clips for five cartons.

**Prices.** Two shops charge $9 and $6 for the same pad. How much does buying eight at the lower price save? Let S be dollars saved. The price difference is $3 per pad. S = 8 × (9 − 6) = **24 dollars**. Check by subtracting the complete bills, $72 − $48 = $24. About $80 less about $50 suggests a saving near $30.

Equal counts gave unequal bills. What if each shop receives $36? Let D be the difference in pad counts. The higher price buys 36 ÷ 9 = 4 pads, the lower buys 36 ÷ 6 = 6. D = 6 − 4 = **2 pads**. Rebuild both bills: 4 × $9 and 6 × $6 each give $36. A difference smaller than either purchase count fits the story.

**Table rental.** Twenty guests arrive on one bus and twelve on another. They sit four per table. What will all the tables cost at $7 each? Let T be the total cost. 20 + 12 = 32 guests. Then 32 guests ÷ 4 guests per table = 8 tables. T = 8 × $7 = **$56**. Check the seats, 8 × 4 = 32, then the bill, $56 ÷ 8 = $7 per table. Rounding the rent to $10 puts the bill below $80. Eight answers the table-count question only.

**Transport.** For the [pilot's 1,205 riders](../quotient-zero-and-capacity.md#find-the-vehicles-needed), let V count four-passenger vehicles needed. 301 full vehicles seat 1,204 riders, leaving one. V = 301 + 1 = **302 vehicles**. Test both capacities: 301 × 4 = 1,204 < 1,205 and 302 × 4 = 1,208 ≥ 1,205. About 300 vehicles fits 1,200 riders. If one rider cancels, exactly 1,204 riders need 301 vehicles. With 300 there are just 1,200 seats. No extra vehicle is required at the exact threshold.

**Packing estimate.** For 247 jars packed six to a case, 240 ÷ 6 gives about 40 cases and 300 ÷ 6 gives about 50. The estimates differ in quality: 247 − 240 = 7 jars, while 300 − 247 = 53 jars. Check exact capacity before ordering. Forty-one cases hold 246 jars, one short. Let B be the cases needed. B = 41 + 1 = **42 cases**, holding 252 jars. For exactly 246 jars, 41 cases suffice.

**Parent bridge.** Label 24 “clips in one carton,” 3 “dollars per pad,” and 8 “tables” before reading the final totals. In the vehicle count, the leftover one names a rider. Seating that rider changes the number of vehicles.
