# How can a tray with no tens give up four tens?

Grade 2 · Place-value exchanges

## Trading a ten in fifty-two

**[2.NBT.B.5](https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/Content/2/NBT/B/5/).** A tray holds 52 sticks. How many remain after 38 are removed? A bar stands for ten, a dot for one. Read each line as a picture of the sticks still on the tray.

```text
Start          |||||    ••
Untie one      ||||     ••••• ••••• ••
Remove 38      |        ••••
```

Five tens and two ones are **50 + 2 = 52**. Untying one bundle gives **40 + 12 = 52**. Lift out three tens and eight ones. Four tens less three tens leaves one ten. Twelve ones less eight ones leaves four ones. **52 − 38 = 14 sticks.**

**Parent bridge.** In familiar column subtraction, crossing out the 5 and writing 4 above the tens records the untied bundle. Writing 12 above the ones counts its ten sticks together with the original two. The marks describe the same trade as the drawing.

For the inverse calculation, 38 + 14 = (30 + 10) + (8 + 4) = 40 + 12 = 52. A second quick addition within 100 is 46 + 27. Move four of the 27 to the 46 to make 50. Then 50 + 23 = **73**. Subtract 20 and then 7 from 73 to return to 53 and then 46. These strategies support fluency within 100. A learner's fluency needs evidence from their own calculations.

## Four bundles of tickets

**[2.NBT.B.6](https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/Content/2/NBT/B/6/) · [2.NBT.B.9](https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/Content/2/NBT/B/9/).** Four ticket packets contain 24, 37, 16 and 23 tickets. How many tickets in all? Open every packet and separate tens from loose tickets.

| Packet | Tens contribution | Ones contribution |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 24 | 20 | 4 |
| 37 | 30 | 7 |
| 16 | 10 | 6 |
| 23 | 20 | 3 |
| Partial sum | 80 | 20 |

Each **partial sum** collects one place from every packet. Addition lets us change the order and grouping of parts. Every ticket still occurs once. The tens give 20 + 30 + 10 + 20 = 80 and the ones give 4 + 7 + 6 + 3 = 20. Combine the two partial sums to get **100 tickets**. Check by grouping whole packets instead. 24 + 16 = 40 and 37 + 23 = 60. The two packet pairs give 100. Remove the packets in reverse order to check the original count. From 100 take 23 to reach 77, then take 16 to reach 61, then 37 to reach 24. The last 24 empties the tray.

## Two trades across the empty tens place

**[2.NBT.B.7](https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/Content/2/NBT/B/7/) · [2.NBT.B.9](https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/Content/2/NBT/B/9/).** A supply cart holds 302 sheets. A class takes 148. How many sheets stay? Each square represents a pack of 100 sheets, each bar a pack of ten and each dot a loose sheet. In a group of bars or dots, a space helps the eye count in fives.

```text
                         Hundreds    Tens             Ones
Start                    □□□                          ••
Open one hundred         □□          ||||| |||||      ••
Open one ten             □□          ||||| ||||       ••••• ••••• ••
Take 148 away            □           |||||            ••••
```

1. The cart starts with 300 + 0 + 2 = 302 sheets. Open one hundred to get **200 + 100 + 2 = 302**, with ten available tens.
2. Open one of those tens. The new counts are two hundreds, nine tens and twelve ones. Their value is **200 + 90 + 12 = 302**.
3. Remove one hundred, four tens and eight ones. The differences by place are 2 − 1 = 1 hundred, 9 − 4 = 5 tens and 12 − 8 = 4 ones. **154 sheets stay.**

Check by adding back the class's sheets. 154 + 148 gives 200 + 90 + 12. Compose ten ones to get 200 + 100 + 2. Compose ten tens to get 300 + 2 = 302. Each trade is reversible.

## Composing a ten and a hundred

**[2.NBT.B.7](https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/Content/2/NBT/B/7/).** How many sheets are in two deliveries of 278 and 146? Use the same tray symbols.

```text
                       Hundreds    Tens              Ones
Delivery of 278        □□          ||||| ||          ••••• •••
Delivery of 146        □           ||||              ••••• •
Together               □□□         ||||| ||||| |     ••••• ••••• ••••
Pack ten ones          □□□         ||||| ||||| ||    ••••
Pack ten tens          □□□□        ||                ••••
```

Like places give 200 + 100 = 300, 70 + 40 = 110 and 8 + 6 = 14. Before any trade the value is 300 + 110 + 14 = 424. After packing ten ones it is 300 + 120 + 4 = 424. After packing ten tens it is 400 + 20 + 4 = **424 sheets**. Check by removing the second delivery in a different order. 424 − 100 = 324, 324 − 40 = 284 and 284 − 6 = 278.

## Moving one ten or one hundred mentally

**[2.NBT.B.8](https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/Content/2/NBT/B/8/).** Picture adding or removing one rod or flat. From 397, adding a rod completes the tenth ten and leaves seven loose. The start of each problem is between 100 and 900 inclusive.

| Starting change | Result | Reverse check |
|---|---:|---|
| 397 + 10 | 407 | 407 − 10 = 397 |
| 402 − 10 | 392 | 392 + 10 = 402 |
| 562 + 100 | 662 | 662 − 100 = 562 |
| 562 − 100 | 462 | 462 + 100 = 562 |
| 900 + 100 | 1,000 | 1,000 − 100 = 900 |
| 100 − 100 | 0 | 0 + 100 = 100 |

Removing a rod from 402 requires opening one of four flats. That leaves three flats, nine rods and the original two loose ones. Starting at 100 and removing the only flat leaves an empty tray.
