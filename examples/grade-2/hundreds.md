## Four flats and six loose ones

**2.NBT.A.1.** Read this tray from left to right. Each square stands for a whole hundred-flat.

| Hundreds | Tens | Ones |
|---|---|---|
| □ □ □ □ | (empty) | ● ● ● ● ● ● |

The four flats give four hundreds, the empty space gives zero tens and the six singles give six ones. Write **406**. To rebuild it independently, lay out forty rods and six ones. Group the rods in tens to recover the four flats. Counting the forty rods gives 400 ones, then six more gives 406.

**Parent bridge.** In 400 + 6 the two terms give all the occupied places. In the numeral 406 the zero holds open the tens place. That keeps the 4 in the hundreds place.

## Counting across a hundred

**2.NBT.A.2.** A stockroom counts loose items, packets of five, rods of ten and flats of one hundred. What totals appear when one more group of the same size arrives each time?

| Size of the next group | Running totals |
|---:|---|
| 1 | 197, 198, 199, 200, 201 |
| 1 | 996, 997, 998, 999, 1,000 |
| 5 | 185, 190, 195, 200, 205 |
| 10 | 377, 387, 397, 407, 417 |
| 100 | 600, 700, 800, 900, 1,000 |

The step stays the same across a change of hundreds digit. For instance, ten more than 397 is three ones to reach 400 and seven more to reach 407. Take away that same group to recover 397. In the last row every new flat adds ten rods, so the tens and ones digits stay zero.

## Reading and rebuilding a number

**2.NBT.A.3.** The stockroom needs a written label, a spoken name and a count by place for the same box. Start in any column and reconstruct the others.

| Numeral | Name | Expanded value by place |
|---:|---|---|
| 406 | four hundred six | 400 + 0 + 6 |
| 460 | four hundred sixty | 400 + 60 + 0 |
| 735 | seven hundred thirty-five | 700 + 30 + 5 |
| 1,000 | one thousand | 1,000 + 0 + 0 + 0 |

Hearing “seven hundred thirty-five,” take seven flats, three rods and five ones. Counting 700, 710, 720, 730 and five more returns 735. At the last label, ten flats exchange for one thousand. The fourth place from the right records that new unit.

## Which tray holds more?

**2.NBT.A.4.** Compare three-digit labels starting with the largest place. Only if two counts agree there do we need the next place.

| Labels | First place that differs | Check by rebuilding in ones |
|---|---|---|
| 406 < 460 | Four hundreds match. Zero tens is less than six tens. | After removing 400 from each, 6 < 60. |
| 503 > 499 | Five hundreds exceeds four hundreds. | 499 needs one to reach 500 and three more to reach 503. |
| 735 > 732 | Both have seven hundreds and three tens. Five ones exceeds two. | Removing 730 from each leaves 5 > 2. |
| 406 = 406 | All three places match. | Both rebuild as 400 + 0 + 6. |

The count and written labels reach 1,000. Each tray comparison here uses two three-digit numbers.
