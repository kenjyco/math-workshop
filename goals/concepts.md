# Concept families and relationships

A family can contain several distinct topics. Use the numbered goal and its example to identify the specific topic, such as the size of a quotient digit, its position, or its transfer into a written answer. [Ordered goals](README.md)

## Meanings across grades

| Concept ID | Meaning and vocabulary to establish | Progression or distinction to preserve |
|---|---|---|
| `count` | Use ordered number words or numerals. *Next, before, after, count on, skip-count*. | Recite a sequence, begin partway through it, then count by specified increments. |
| `card` | Cardinality is how many members a collection has. One-to-one correspondence pairs one word with one object. *Set, total, none, zero*. | Reordering or spacing the same objects preserves quantity. |
| `order` | Compare magnitudes against a common meaning or unit. *Greater, less, equal, fewer, more, >, <, =*. | Collections and numerals → digit values → fractions and decimals with a common whole. |
| `add` | Addition joins quantities. Subtraction measures removal, a missing part, or an additive difference. *Addend, sum, difference, make ten*. | Distinguish the unknown's role from where it appears in an equation. |
| `equal` | An equation asserts both sides have the same value. A symbol can stand for an unknown. *Equals, expression, equation, true, false*. | Use totals on either side, then boxes and letters. |
| `place` | A digit's value is its digit value times its place unit. Ten of one whole-number unit make one of the next. *Ones, tens, hundreds, thousands, digit, numeral, expanded form*. | Ten individual ones → one composite ten → nested units, internal zeros, and values through one million. |
| `exchange` | Regrouping trades equal quantities in different units. *Compose, decompose, regroup, trade, partial sum*. Partial sums add selected place-value parts before combining. | Relate “carry” or “borrow,” when familiar, to an explicit equal-value trade. |
| `round` | Replace a number by a nearest multiple of the selected place unit. *Estimate, nearest, midpoint, benchmark*. | Tens/hundreds → any relevant place. An estimate provides a magnitude check. |
| `groups` | Equal groups have the same number in each. A rectangular array arranges entries in rows and columns. *Pair, even, odd, row, column, equal addends*. | Counting groups, pairing, repeated addition, and area tiling have distinct questions. |
| `product` | For whole-number equal groups, multiplying group count by group size gives a product. *Multiply, factor, times, times as many*. | Extend from equal groups to rectangular products and multiplicative comparison. |
| `divide` | Division with a nonzero divisor finds the missing factor in an exact product, or a whole-number quotient and remainder. *Dividend, divisor, quotient, remainder, share, group size*. | Preserve both sharing among a known number of groups and forming groups of a known size. |
| `laws` | Commutativity exchanges operands of addition or multiplication. Associativity regroups them. Distribution multiplies each part of a sum and combines the products. | Act out the property before requiring terminology. Subtraction and division cannot generally exchange operands. |
| `multi-product` | A multidigit product can be assembled from products of place-value parts. *Expanded form, partial product, area model, ones row, tens row*. | One-digit × tens → up to four digits × one digit and two digits × two digits → grade 5 algorithm fluency. |
| `multi-div` | Partition a multidigit dividend by place value or subtract whole multiples of the divisor. *Partial quotient, quotient place, remainder*. | Preserve zero positions and complete the final check. Remainder interpretation belongs to the story. |
| `factor` | A positive factor divides a positive integer exactly. A factor pair multiplies to it. A multiple is the corresponding product. A prime has exactly two positive factors, a composite more than two. | Grade 4 works in 1–100. One has exactly one positive factor. A square's equal-factor pair occurs once. |
| `pattern` | A rule generates or organizes terms, and properties can explain features of the result. *Rule, term, repeat, increase, odd, even*. | Separate describing displayed terms from explaining why a feature continues. |
| `model` | Represent quantities and their relationships so that a calculation answers the original question. *Known, unknown, step, estimate, check, unit*. | Actions and drawings → equations with symbols → multistep interpretation. |
| `length` | Length measures a span by iterating an equal unit. *Endpoint, longer, shorter, distance, ruler, inch, centimeter*. | Direct comparison → indirect comparison → whole, then fractional standard units. |
| `scale` | A marked scale assigns values to positions using equal intervals for equal changes. *Origin, interval, tick, baseline, number line*. | Count spaces between marks. Read the reference unit before reading a position. |
| `time` | A clock reading locates a time of day. Elapsed time measures an interval. *Hour, half-hour, minute, second, a.m., p.m.* | Hour/half-hour → five minutes → minute and elapsed minutes → conversion and applications. |
| `money` | Monetary values can be combined or compared in a common unit. *Dollar, cent, bill, coin, quarter, dime, nickel, penny*. | Coin values need not correspond to physical coin sizes. One dollar equals 100 cents. |
| `units` | A measurement states a number and the unit for an attribute. Equivalent measurements express the same quantity. *Mass, weight, liquid volume, capacity, larger/smaller unit, conversion*. | Compare everyday weights in K. Measure mass in g/kg and liquid volume in L in grade 3. Convert within a system in grade 4. |
| `data` | Observations may be grouped by category or value and represented by their frequencies. *Category, tally, frequency, picture graph, bar graph, key*. | Count and sort → compare categories → scale a key and answer multistep questions. |
| `plot` | A measurement line plot places one mark above its measured value for each observation. *Horizontal scale, observation, frequency*. | Whole units → halves/quarters → eighths and arithmetic read from data. |
| `shape` | Classify figures by defining properties. *Side, vertex/corner, face, edge, plane figure, solid, triangle, quadrilateral, rectangle, square, rhombus, pentagon, hexagon*. | Use accurate models of solids and varied orientations. A square has four equal sides and four right angles, so it satisfies both rectangle and rhombus definitions. |
| `compose` | Combine pieces into a figure, or decompose a figure into pieces. *Part, whole, composite, boundary*. | A shared internal edge can disappear from the outside boundary. Composition alone does not imply equal parts. |
| `share` | Partition one whole into parts equal in the quantity being considered. *Half, third, fourth/quarter, equal share*. | For regions, check equal area. Equal-area pieces can have different shapes. |
| `fraction` | For a fixed unit whole and positive denominator b, a/b counts a units of size 1/b. *Unit fraction, numerator, denominator, whole, number line*. | Name the whole explicitly and allow amounts equal to or larger than one. |
| `equiv` | Equivalent fractions are names for the same magnitude. *Equivalent, same point, subdivide, common denominator, benchmark*. | Special cases → explain uniform subdivision → compare different numerators and denominators. |
| `frac-add` | Add or subtract counts of like fractional units. *Like denominator, decompose, mixed number, exchange a whole*. | Grade 4 includes mixed numbers and the tenths/hundredths connection. |
| `frac-mul` | A whole-number multiplier repeats a fractional quantity. *Multiple of a unit fraction, copies, product*. | n copies of a/b give na copies of 1/b. General fraction-by-fraction products are future work. |
| `decimal` | Decimal notation gives tenths and hundredths place values to the right of the ones place. *Decimal point, tenth, hundredth, equivalent decimal*. | Match decimal, fraction, and grid or line using the same whole. A final zero can preserve value. |
| `area` | Area measures the extent of a plane region in square units. *Unit square, square unit, tile, rectangle, rectilinear, additive*. | Exact covering → rows × columns → distributive and composite areas → rectangle formula. |
| `perimeter` | Perimeter is the total length around a closed figure. *Boundary, side length, missing side*. | Count only the exterior sides. Numerical area alone cannot determine perimeter. |
| `angle` | Two rays with a common endpoint form an angle. Its measure describes the selected turn. *Point, line, segment, ray, vertex, degree, acute, right, obtuse, parallel, perpendicular*. | A line extends both ways, a ray one way, and a segment has two endpoints. In a plane, distinct parallel lines never meet. Perpendicular lines meet at right angles. |
| `symmetry` | A reflection axis maps a whole figure onto itself. *Fold line, mirror image, line of symmetry*. | Equal areas on the two sides are insufficient. Corresponding points must match under reflection. |

Read down the source columns to find possible next ideas, then read the full target row for its other foundations. Children may explore any goal directly.

## Related ideas

Each connection works in both directions.

| Related IDs | Reason to follow the link |
|---|---|
| `area` ↔ `perimeter` | Measure the inside and the boundary of the same figure. |
| `pattern` ↔ `factor` | Multiples recur at predictable intervals in number and product tables. |
| `decimal` ↔ `money` | Hundredths of a dollar provide one familiar decimal context. |
| `angle` ↔ `time` | Clock hands provide turns around a shared center. |
| `groups` ↔ `compose` | A collection or figure can be reorganized into visible parts while preserving its total. |
