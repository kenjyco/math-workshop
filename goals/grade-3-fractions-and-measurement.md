## G35. Unit fractions in regions and on a number line

Concepts `share`, `area`, `fraction`, `scale`. Use [G16](grade-1.md#g16-halves-and-fourths-of-circles-and-rectangles) and [G21](grade-2.md#g21-classifying-figures-and-checking-equal-shares) for equal shares, [G23](grade-2.md#g23-representing-length-arithmetic-on-a-number-line) for intervals and [G32](grade-3-operations-and-area.md#g32-measuring-with-square-units-and-adding-rectangle-areas) for equal areas. With one fixed whole, a denominator counts equal parts in one whole. The numerator counts how many such parts are taken.

| Standard | Grade expectation | Selected evidence |
|---|---|---|
| [3.G.A.2](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=26) | Partition shapes into equal-area parts and express each part as a unit fraction of the whole area. | Label one of three equal-area parts 1/3. Reassemble and verify the full area. |
| [3.NF.A.1](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=24) | Interpret 1/b as one of b equal parts of a whole and a/b as a such parts. | Name the whole, partition it equally, then iterate a part. Verify the size of one part and the count taken. |
| [3.NF.A.2](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=24) | Represent a fraction as a number on a number line. | Show the unit interval and a labeled point. Check the point's distance from zero. |
| [3.NF.A.2.a](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=24) | Partition 0–1 into b equal intervals and locate 1/b at the end of the first. | Make a sixths line. Verify six intervals and seven boundary marks. |
| [3.NF.A.2.b](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=24) | Iterate a lengths of 1/b from zero to locate a/b. | Place 5/4, extending the same scale beyond one. Check five quarter-lengths. |

**Examples.** Partition a region into equal areas, name each as a unit fraction, then mark the fixed unit 0–1 on a line. Locate a unit fraction, several copies, and a point beyond one. Keep grade 3 fractional units to halves, thirds, fourths, sixths and eighths.

**Independent check.** MP6. Verify equal areas by recomposition. Check six equal intervals against seven boundary marks, and locate 5/4 by measuring five successive quarter-lengths from zero. A reviewer should recover the fraction from the drawing with the point label covered.

**Parent bridge.** After the region and line, connect the size of a single share to the length of a single jump. The fraction names a number at the resulting point.

## G36. Equivalent names and fraction comparisons

Concepts `equiv`, `order`, `scale`, `fraction`. [G35](grade-3-fractions-and-measurement.md#g35-unit-fractions-in-regions-and-on-a-number-line) supplies fractional units and a fixed whole, [G08](grade-1.md#g08-making-ten-finding-a-missing-addend-and-testing-equality) equality and [G02](kindergarten.md#g02-comparing-collections-and-sorting-by-count) order. Equivalent names denote the same magnitude. A common numerator holds the count fixed. A common denominator holds unit size fixed.

| Standard | Grade expectation | Selected evidence |
|---|---|---|
| [3.NF.A.3](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=24) | Explain simple equivalence and compare fractions through size. | Use equal-length wholes and a common line to justify equality and inequality. |
| [3.NF.A.3.a](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=24) | Recognize equal fractions by equal magnitude or coincident number-line points. | Align two partitions. Check the endpoint coincides with a shared unit. |
| [3.NF.A.3.b](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=24) | Recognize, generate, and explain simple equivalent fractions. | Subdivide each third into two equal parts. Check that the highlighted length stays fixed. |
| [3.NF.A.3.c](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=24) | Express whole numbers as fractions and recognize fractions equal to whole numbers. | Locate 3/1=3, 4/4=1, and 8/4=2. Check with full unit intervals. |
| [3.NF.A.3.d](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=24) | Compare fractions with common numerator or common denominator using the same whole. Record >, =, < and justify. | Include each comparison type. Verify part size as well as number of parts. |

**Examples.** Build 2/3 and 4/6 on the same line by subdividing thirds. Rename a whole number with denominator one and identify fractions equal to whole numbers. Compare same-numerator and same-denominator pairs on equal wholes. Include equality and both inequality signs. Use grade 3 denominators 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, plus the explicit whole-number-over-one case.

**Independent check.** MP3. Verify the highlighted endpoint from each partition and the common 0–1 length. Contrast a misleading drawing with unequal wholes. Check a comparison by explaining which is greater from part count or part size, as appropriate.

**Parent bridge.** After the coincident points, show why adding boundary marks changes the fraction's name while preserving its location.

## G37. Measuring elapsed minutes across an hour

Concepts `time`, `scale`. [G24](grade-2.md#g24-five-minute-clock-readings-and-times-of-day) supplies the clock and [G23](grade-2.md#g23-representing-length-arithmetic-on-a-number-line) a marked interval. A time of day is a location. Elapsed minutes measure a duration.

| Standard | Grade expectation | Selected evidence |
|---|---|---|
| [3.MD.A.1](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=24) | Read/write time to the minute, measure intervals in minutes, and solve additive elapsed-time stories. | Cross an hour boundary. Add the interval back to the start time. |

**Examples.** Read and write time to the nearest minute. Use clocks and a timeline from 3:48 to 4:07, then pose both addition and subtraction interval problems including a missing start time.

**Independent check.** MP5. Justify the timeline for counting across the hour. A reviewer should recover both endpoints by independent forward and backward moves and confirm 19 elapsed minutes in the anchor case.

**Parent bridge.** After the hour crossing, connect the split at 4:00 to regrouping 60 minutes as one hour.

## G38. Measuring mass and liquid volume and operating in one unit

Concepts `units`, `model`. Use [G22](grade-2.md#g22-selecting-a-length-tool-estimating-and-measuring) for benchmarks, [G23](grade-2.md#g23-representing-length-arithmetic-on-a-number-line) for marked scales and [G27](grade-3-operations-and-area.md#g27-products-both-meanings-of-division-and-missing-factors) for equal groups. Grams and kilograms measure mass. Liters measure liquid volume.

| Standard | Grade expectation | Selected evidence |
|---|---|---|
| [3.MD.A.2](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=24) | Measure and estimate mass in g/kg and liquid volume in liters. Solve one-step same-unit stories across the four operations. Exclude compound units, geometric container volume, and multiplicative comparisons. | Read a balance or marked vessel, give a plausible estimate, then solve a same-unit story. Verify scale and answer unit. |

**Examples.** Include actual measuring specifications and estimates with g, kg and L, using an appropriate balance or marked vessel. Use all four operations across one-step stories with measurements in the same units. Use equal groups for multiplication or division.

**Independent check.** MP5. Independently read each marked interval and justify a plausible benchmark. Reconstruct totals after equal sharing and check that the answer unit matches the quantity asked for. Exclude compound units such as cubic centimeters, geometric container volume and multiplicative comparisons.

**Parent bridge.** After the measurement, relate the label on the scale to the unit that must accompany the answer.

## G39. Interpreting scaled picture and bar graphs

Concepts `data`, `scale`. [G26](grade-2.md#g26-recording-measurements-and-graphing-categories) supplies category frequencies and graphs. [G27](grade-3-operations-and-area.md#g27-products-both-meanings-of-division-and-missing-factors) supplies equal groups. A scale factor gives the number represented by each symbol or interval of bar height.

| Standard | Grade expectation | Selected evidence |
|---|---|---|
| [3.MD.B.3](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=25) | Draw scaled picture and bar graphs for several categories. Use scaled bars to solve one- and two-step comparison questions. | Choose a non-unit key. Reconstruct the source counts before answering. |

**Examples.** Draw both scaled graphs from one multi-category data set. Use a non-unit key and a labeled baseline. Ask one- and two-step “how many more/fewer?” questions using the bar graph.

**Independent check.** MP2. Independently multiply the symbol count or bar intervals by the key to recover the data, then check an answer by comparing reconstructed counts. Include a case where counting symbols as individual observations gives a different result.

**Parent bridge.** After the graphs, connect one drawn symbol to one equal group of observations.

## G40. Measuring quarter inches and plotting repeated lengths

Concepts `length`, `plot`. [G22](grade-2.md#g22-selecting-a-length-tool-estimating-and-measuring) supplies ruler alignment, [G26](grade-2.md#g26-recording-measurements-and-graphing-categories) the observation-to-mark rule and [G35](grade-3-fractions-and-measurement.md#g35-unit-fractions-in-regions-and-on-a-number-line) fractional intervals. A quarter inch is one of four equal intervals per inch.

| Standard | Grade expectation | Selected evidence |
|---|---|---|
| [3.MD.B.4](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=25) | Measure lengths with rulers marked in halves and fourths of an inch. Plot data on an appropriate whole-, half-, or quarter-unit scale. | Retain measured values, including repeats. Check ruler origin, spacing, and plot frequencies. |

**Examples.** Generate length data with rulers marked in halves and fourths of an inch. Choose a whole-, half- or quarter-unit plot scale appropriate to the values. Include a repeat at a fractional position and a position with no observations.

**Independent check.** MP6. Have a reviewer recover original measurements from the plotted marks, including multiplicities, then check the ruler endpoints and number of equal intervals independently. Label the unit on both ruler and plot.

**Parent bridge.** After the repeated measure, distinguish the spacing along the axis from the number of marks stacked above it.
