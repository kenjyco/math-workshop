# Grade 2

Distinguish mental fact recall, fluent calculation within 100, and modeled calculation within 1,000. For sums and differences within a limit, the sum or starting amount in subtraction stays within that limit. ## G17. Mental facts and one- and two-step additive stories

Concepts `model`, `add`, `equal`. Use [G08](grade-1.md#g08-making-ten-finding-a-missing-addend-and-testing-equality) for mental strategies and [G09](grade-1.md#g09-finding-the-unknown-in-an-additive-story) for situation types. An intermediate result supplies a quantity for the next step. Inverse operations can recover an original part.

| Standard | Grade expectation | Selected evidence |
|---|---|---|
| [2.OA.A.1](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=19) | Solve one- and two-step additive stories within 100, across Table 1 situations and unknown positions. | Vary start, change, result, part, and compared quantities. Check both operations in a two-step story. |
| [2.OA.B.2](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=19) | Add and subtract mentally and fluently within 20. By year end recall every sum of two one-digit numbers. | Use grade 1 mental strategies to derive facts, then sample varied recall cases. Account for the full recall set. Verify inverse differences separately. |

**Examples.** Separate mental fluency within 20 and year-end recall of sums of two one-digit numbers from story calculations within 100. Reuse the Table 1 situations and unknowns in G09 at this range. Choose a story step whose missing part can be found from a known sum. Show how that fact supplies the next quantity. Represent the stories with objects or drawings and equations with a symbol. Include a two-step comparison whose first answer is a subtotal.

**Independent check.** MP1. Have a reviewer work backward from the requested quantity and test both equations. Include a compact record spanning all one-digit addend pairs, including zero, with inverse differences and within-20 cases outside the recall subset.

**Parent bridge.** After the two-step story, label the subtotal with its own question and the final answer with the original question.

## G18. Making hundreds, counting in increments and comparing

Concepts `place`, `exchange`, `count`, `pattern`, `order`. [G10](grade-1.md#g10-reading-tens-and-ones-and-counting-through-120) supplies tens and place-based comparison. Ten tens make a hundred. Expanded form adds the values of occupied positions. A zero can hold an empty position.

| Standard | Grade expectation | Selected evidence |
|---|---|---|
| [2.NBT.A.1](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=19) | Interpret three-digit numbers by hundreds, tens, and ones. | Build 406. Verify the empty tens column and expanded value. |
| [2.NBT.A.1.a](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=19) | Ten tens form one hundred. | Trade ten ten-rods for one hundred-flat. Verify both have 100 ones. |
| [2.NBT.A.1.b](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=19) | Interpret 100 through 900 by hundreds with zero tens and ones. | Match drawings to exact hundreds. Check all three digit positions. |
| [2.NBT.A.2](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=19) | Count within 1,000 and skip-count by 5, 10, and 100. | Cross a hundred boundary in each applicable sequence. Check constant increments. |
| [2.NBT.A.3](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=19) | Read and write numbers to 1,000 as numerals, words, and expanded form. | Convert among the three forms, including zeros and 1,000. Sum expanded parts. |
| [2.NBT.A.4](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=19) | Compare three-digit numbers by digit value using >, =, <. | Inspect the highest differing place. Include equality. Verify with expanded values. |

**Examples.** Build 406 and exact hundreds from 100 to 900. Translate words, numerals and expanded forms through 1,000. Count within 1,000 by ones and skip-count by fives, tens and hundreds. Include sequences crossing a hundred.

**Independent check.** MP7. Rebuild values independently in ones. Check the first differing place in each comparison, including 406 versus 460 and equal three-digit numbers. End a count at 1,000 while keeping comparison operands three digits.

**Parent bridge.** After the empty tens column, connect the model to the place kept by the zero when the expanded sum is written as a numeral.

## G19. Adding partial sums and exchanging across a zero

Concepts `add`, `exchange`, `laws`, `place`. Use [G11](grade-1.md#g11-adding-and-subtracting-in-tens-and-ones) for equal-value trades, [G18](grade-2.md#g18-making-hundreds-counting-in-increments-and-comparing) for hundreds and [G17](grade-2.md#g17-mental-facts-and-one--and-two-step-additive-stories) for facts. A partial sum can collect like place units from several addends. Decomposing a hundred produces ten tens of the same value.

| Standard | Grade expectation | Selected evidence |
|---|---|---|
| [2.NBT.B.5](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=19) | Fluently add and subtract within 100 using place value, properties, or the inverse relationship. | Include crossing a ten in each direction. Check by inverse or recomposition. |
| [2.NBT.B.6](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=19) | Add up to four two-digit numbers using place value and properties. | Group tens and ones across four addends. Verify no addend disappeared. |
| [2.NBT.B.7](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=19) | Add and subtract within 1,000 with concrete or drawn models linked to writing. Compose or decompose tens and hundreds when needed. | Show a trade across a zero in subtraction and a composition in addition. Value-check each exchange. |
| [2.NBT.B.8](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=19) | Mentally add or subtract 10 or 100 from a starting number in 100–900. | Include 900 + 100 and 100 − 100. Check the limit on the **starting** number. |
| [2.NBT.B.9](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=19) | Explain additive strategies by place value and operation properties. Drawings or objects may support the explanation. | Explain why partial sums recombine. Match each subtotal to the original addends. |

**Examples.** Include fluent calculations within 100, addition of four two-digit numbers, and written methods linked to concrete or drawn models for addition and subtraction within 1,000. Use 52 − 38 for a ten exchange and 302 − 148 for two linked exchanges. Separately show mental changes of ±10 and ±100. Include a sum that composes both a ten and a hundred.

**Independent check.** MP7. Independently verify that every renaming still sums to the minuend, then add the difference back. In four-addend sums, check each input once in each occupied place. Test mental changes at starts 100 and 900.

**Parent bridge.** After 52 − 38, map the crossed-out tens digit and the increased ones count in column subtraction to the same physical exchange.

## G20. Pairs, equal addends and square arrays

Concepts `groups`, `add`, `area`. Use [G01](kindergarten.md#g01-counting-a-sequence-and-counting-a-collection) for counting, [G05](kindergarten.md#g05-joining-separating-and-splitting-a-whole) for addition and [G07](kindergarten.md#g07-building-figures-and-composing-a-new-shape) for a rectangle's boundary. A pair has two members. An even collection can be paired completely or split into two equal groups. An array has rows and columns.

| Standard | Grade expectation | Selected evidence |
|---|---|---|
| [2.OA.C.3](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=19) | Determine parity for groups up to 20. Express an even count as two equal addends. | Pair counters for adjacent odd/even totals. Verify the leftover and the even equation. |
| [2.OA.C.4](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=19) | Total rectangular arrays up to five rows by five columns using addition. Write the equal-addend equation. | Mark complete rows and write one addend for each. Recount all entries. |
| [2.G.A.2](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=20) | Split a rectangle into rows and columns of same-size squares and count them. | Complete a grid. Check congruent square cells, complete coverage, and total. |

**Examples.** Pair odd and even counts through 20 and express an even count as two equal addends. In arrays up to five by five, sum one equal addend per row. Partition a rectangle into equal square cells and count them. Distinguish dots at positions from cells that cover a region.

**Independent check.** MP7. Turn a non-square array and check both row equations against a direct count. In the tiled model, count square interiors independently of the grid intersections. Include a single row.

**Parent bridge.** After the array, connect the written equal addends to the rows. Product notation can wait.

## G21. Classifying figures and checking equal shares

Concepts `shape`, `share`. [G15](grade-1.md#g15-defining-attributes-and-composing-in-two-stages) supplies defining attributes and [G16](grade-1.md#g16-halves-and-fourths-of-circles-and-rectangles) supplies equal shares. Side count classifies polygons. A cube has six equal square faces. Equal shares refer to equal area.

| Standard | Grade expectation | Selected evidence |
|---|---|---|
| [2.G.A.1](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=20) | Recognize and draw figures with specified attributes. Identify triangles, quadrilaterals, pentagons, hexagons, and cubes. | Classify by angles, sides, or equal faces as appropriate. Use direct or visual size comparisons. Measurement is unnecessary here. |
| [2.G.A.3](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=20) | Divide circles and rectangles into two, three, or four equal shares. Name shares and wholes. Equal shares of identical wholes may have different shapes. | Compare diagonal and midline halves of identical rectangles. Verify equal area in each and rebuild the wholes. |

**Examples.** Identify and draw shapes with given attributes such as a specified number of angles. Identify cubes using equal faces. Partition circles and rectangles into halves, thirds and fourths. Compare identical rectangles partitioned into halves by a diagonal and by a midline. Classify the resulting triangles and rectangles by their sides and angles before comparing their areas.

**Independent check.** MP3. Check the named properties by inspection or direct comparison. Superpose or rearrange pieces to justify equal area even when corresponding shares have different shapes. Name each whole in terms of its shares.

**Parent bridge.** After the two rectangles, show how two different-looking halves each occupy half of an identical whole.

## G22. Selecting a length tool, estimating and measuring

Concepts `length`, `scale`, `units`, `add`. Use [G12](grade-1.md#g12-comparing-length-indirectly-and-measuring-by-iteration) for endpoints and iterating units. A standard unit has a fixed size. An estimate uses a benchmark. A measured difference gives the extra span.

| Standard | Grade expectation | Selected evidence |
|---|---|---|
| [2.MD.A.1](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=20) | Select and use an appropriate measuring tool for length. | Choose among ruler, yardstick, meter stick, or tape for specified objects. Verify alignment and unit. |
| [2.MD.A.2](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=20) | Measure the same object with two different-size units and relate unit size to the numerical result. | Cover one span with two unit sizes. Check fixed endpoints and why smaller units require more copies. |
| [2.MD.A.3](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=20) | Estimate lengths in inches, feet, centimeters, and meters. | Use a familiar benchmark for each unit, then measure. Report estimate and measurement with units. |
| [2.MD.A.4](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=20) | Measure how much longer one object is than another in a standard unit. | Align or measure two strips and subtract. Add the difference back to the shorter length. |

**Examples.** Choose tools for a page, room and curved path. Measure one fixed object twice with different-length units. Estimate lengths in inches, feet, centimeters and meters against appropriate benchmarks and compare estimates with measurements.

**Independent check.** MP5. Justify tool choice from span and flexibility. Independently measure the extra length of the longer of two objects and recover the longer measurement by addition. Count the intervals when a strip spans marks 2 through 8 cm.

**Parent bridge.** After the shifted start, connect ruler subtraction to the six unit intervals. A printed endpoint alone gives length only from zero.

## G23. Representing length arithmetic on a number line

Concepts `length`, `model`, `scale`, `add`. [G22](grade-2.md#g22-selecting-a-length-tool-estimating-and-measuring) provides standard-unit lengths and [G17](grade-2.md#g17-mental-facts-and-one--and-two-step-additive-stories) provides same-unit additive stories. A position marks the distance from zero. An interval has length between marks.

| Standard | Grade expectation | Selected evidence |
|---|---|---|
| [2.MD.B.5](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=20) | Solve same-unit length stories using addition or subtraction within 100. | Show a combined or missing length on a labeled sketch. Check units throughout. |
| [2.MD.B.6](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=20) | Show whole numbers as lengths from zero and sums/differences within 100 on an equally spaced number line. | Label zero, unit intervals, start, movement, and endpoint. Count intervals. |

**Examples.** Solve combined and missing-length problems within 100 with all lengths in one unit, using a sketch and an equation with an unknown. Draw a number line starting at zero, with equally spaced ticks for consecutive whole numbers. Show jumps in both directions.

**Independent check.** MP4. Read start and end before reading the equation. Count the spaces jumped and verify a reverse jump returns to the start. Include a start away from zero so that distance moved differs from the ending label.

**Parent bridge.** After the reverse jump, connect the two endpoints and the difference to three lengths in the story.

## G24. Five-minute clock readings and times of day

Concepts `time`. [G13](grade-1.md#g13-reading-hours-and-half-hours) gives the roles of the two hands. [G18](grade-2.md#g18-making-hundreds-counting-in-increments-and-comparing) supports counting by fives. A five-minute interval corresponds to one hour-number spacing for the minute hand. The labels a.m. and p.m. locate a time within a day.

| Standard | Grade expectation | Selected evidence |
|---|---|---|
| [2.MD.C.7](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=20) | Read and write analog and digital time to the nearest five minutes using a.m. and p.m. | Pair a face and display with a morning or evening event. Verify hand positions and designation. |

**Examples.** Read and write analog and digital times to the nearest five minutes, including a near-hour time and a morning/evening pair with the same hand positions.

**Independent check.** MP6. Reconstruct both hand positions from each display and check the hour hand approaches the next numeral at :55. Use the event description to select a.m. or p.m.

**Parent bridge.** After the paired faces, point out which information comes from the hands and which comes from the story.

## G25. Counting money and solving purchase stories

Concepts `money`, `add`. [G17](grade-2.md#g17-mental-facts-and-one--and-two-step-additive-stories) supplies additive totals and differences. A denomination specifies a monetary value. Write dollars with $ before the number and cents with ¢ after the number. A dollar is worth 100 cents.

| Standard | Grade expectation | Selected evidence |
|---|---|---|
| [2.MD.C.8](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=20) | Solve money stories with dollar bills, quarters, dimes, nickels, and pennies. Use dollar and cent symbols correctly. | Count mixed coins and solve a purchase or difference. Verify values, units, and symbol placement. |

**Examples.** Use dollar bills, quarters, dimes, nickels and pennies across purchase or change stories. Match symbols to units. Include equal values made with different coins and a comparison for which coin size would be misleading.

**Independent check.** MP2. Independently exchange both collections into cents and reconstruct the original amount from price plus change. Use whole-dollar or whole-cent notation suited to the example.

**Parent bridge.** After the equal collections, show how a coin exchange can change the number of coins while preserving their total value.

## G26. Recording measurements and graphing categories

Concepts `plot`, `length`, `data`, `model`. [G14](grade-1.md#g14-answering-questions-from-category-displays) supplies categorical observations. [G22](grade-2.md#g22-selecting-a-length-tool-estimating-and-measuring) and [G23](grade-2.md#g23-representing-length-arithmetic-on-a-number-line) supply measurement and an equally spaced scale. A line plot stacks observations at measured values. A key states what a picture represents.

| Standard | Grade expectation | Selected evidence |
|---|---|---|
| [2.MD.D.9](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=20) | Generate length data to the nearest whole unit and make a whole-unit line plot. Repeated measurements of one object are also possible. | Retain the original observations. Check one mark per observation, including duplicates. |
| [2.MD.D.10](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=20) | Create picture and single-unit bar graphs for up to four categories. Solve simple part/whole and comparison problems using the bar graph. | Draw both graphs from one list. Check the key, baseline, counts, and answers. |

**Examples.** Generate length observations to the nearest whole unit. Plot 4, 6 and 6 cm with an empty position for 5. For a second view of the same strips, classify them by a recorded attribute such as material. Use up to four categories for a picture graph and a single-unit bar graph. Use the bar graph for put-together, take-apart and compare questions.

**Independent check.** MP6. Have a reviewer recover all three lengths including the duplicate. From the categorical graphs, reconstruct category counts, total them, and check a difference. Preserve empty intervals on the measurement axis.

**Parent bridge.** After the repeated measurement, explain why two marks occupy the same position. In the bar graph each unit of height instead records one observation in its category.
