## G57. Applying rectangle formulas to known and missing measures

Concepts `area`, `perimeter`, `equal`. [G32](grade-3-operations-and-area.md#g32-measuring-with-square-units-and-adding-rectangle-areas) establishes tiled area, [G33](grade-3-operations-and-area.md#g33-tracing-perimeter-and-comparing-it-with-area) boundary length, and [G27](grade-3-operations-and-area.md#g27-products-both-meanings-of-division-and-missing-factors) missing factors. Length and width are side measures of a rectangle. Area uses their product and perimeter counts two of each.

| Standard | Grade expectation | Selected evidence |
|---|---|---|
| [4.MD.A.3](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=31) | Apply rectangle area and perimeter formulas in applied and mathematical problems. | Find a missing width from area and length, then perimeter. Check linear versus square units. |

**Examples.** Use A = l × w and P = 2 × (l + w) in real and purely mathematical examples. Derive a missing width from area and length, find perimeter, then include a reverse perimeter problem with one side unknown.

**Independent check.** MP4. Independently tile or count rows for area and trace all four sides for perimeter. Substitute recovered dimensions into the original constraints and check that the answer unit matches the requested quantity.

**Parent bridge.** After the missing-width example, connect dividing the area into equal rows to undoing the side product.

## G58. Drawing lines and measuring turns

Concepts `shape`, `angle`, `fraction`, `scale`. [G03](kindergarten.md#g03-naming-shapes-and-locating-them-in-space) supplies plane shapes, [G35](grade-3-fractions-and-measurement.md#g35-unit-fractions-in-regions-and-on-a-number-line) fractional parts and [G23](grade-2.md#g23-representing-length-arithmetic-on-a-number-line) interval scales. A point marks a position. Lines extend both ways, segments have two endpoints and rays extend one way. An angle is formed by two rays at a common endpoint.

| Standard | Grade expectation | Selected evidence |
|---|---|---|
| [4.G.A.1](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=32) | Draw and identify points, lines, segments, rays, right/acute/obtuse angles, and parallel/perpendicular lines in plane figures. | Use endpoint dots and extension arrows consistently. Check the same features inside a figure. |
| [4.MD.C.5](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=31) | Recognize an angle from two rays sharing an endpoint and understand angle measure. | Draw rays, vertex, and selected turn. Verify that changing drawn ray lengths preserves the turn. |
| [4.MD.C.5.a](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=31) | Relate angle to a fraction of a circle centered at the vertex. One degree is 1/360 of a full turn. | Mark a quarter turn in a circle. Recover 90° from the fraction of a full turn. |
| [4.MD.C.5.b](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=31) | A turn spanning n one-degree units measures n degrees. | Accumulate adjacent degree intervals. Check intervals and degree notation. |
| [4.MD.C.6](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=32) | Measure angles to whole-number degrees with a protractor and sketch angles of specified measure. | Align center and baseline, select the scale starting at zero, then measure a constructed angle. |

**Examples.** Draw and identify the named features independently and inside figures. Include acute, right and obtuse angles and parallel and perpendicular lines. Use the arc intercepted by the rays on a circle centered at their vertex. One part in 360 of the full turn measures one degree. Measure to whole degrees with a protractor and sketch specified measures from both baseline directions. Define acute as less than a right angle but greater than zero, right as 90°, and obtuse as between 90° and 180°.

**Independent check.** MP5. Align the tool's center and zero baseline, identify the intended turn and count its degree intervals. Independently measure a sketch from the other ray. Verify a quarter-turn is 90° and that lengthening the drawn rays preserves the angle.

**Parent bridge.** After the protractor pair, connect its two printed scales to the two possible starting rays. Preserve the same vertex and zero throughout a reading.

## G59. Finding an unknown angle from adjacent turns

Concepts `angle`, `add`, `equal`. [G58](grade-4-geometry.md#g58-drawing-lines-and-measuring-turns) supplies degrees and the vertex, [G08](grade-1.md#g08-making-ten-finding-a-missing-addend-and-testing-equality) equality and [G05](kindergarten.md#g05-joining-separating-and-splitting-a-whole) part–whole addition. Angle measures add when adjacent parts share a vertex and have disjoint interiors.

| Standard | Grade expectation | Selected evidence |
|---|---|---|
| [4.MD.C.7](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=32) | Use additivity of adjacent, non-overlapping angle parts to find unknowns in diagrams and applied problems. | Mark the whole turn and its parts. Substitute the missing angle and verify their sum. |

**Examples.** Split a right angle with one part 32°. Include an unknown angle in a second mathematical diagram and an applied turn problem. Represent a missing part or whole with a symbol in an equation. Contrast an overlapping pair where the sum would count one turn twice.

**Independent check.** MP3. A reviewer should reconstruct the whole from its marked parts, independently measure a to-scale diagram and verify 58° for the missing part of the right angle. Use stated measures for schematic drawings.

**Parent bridge.** After the split turn, connect addition of adjacent angles to counting each one-degree interval once.

## G60. Classifying figures by lines and angle sizes

Concepts `shape`, `angle`. [G34](grade-3-operations-and-area.md#g34-classifying-quadrilaterals-by-shared-attributes) supplies classification by attributes and [G58](grade-4-geometry.md#g58-drawing-lines-and-measuring-turns) supplies line relationships and angle measures. Parallel lines in a plane never meet. Perpendicular lines meet at right angles. A right triangle has a right angle.

| Standard | Grade expectation | Selected evidence |
|---|---|---|
| [4.G.A.2](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=32) | Classify plane figures by presence or absence of stated line relationships or angle sizes. Identify right triangles. | Sort rotated figures, including right triangles and near misses. Verify the defining property. |

**Examples.** Classify plane figures by presence or absence of parallel or perpendicular lines and by angles of a specified size. Include a rotated right triangle and a near-right triangle. Give geometric evidence sufficient to distinguish them.

**Independent check.** MP3. Independently test each proposed classification using markings or measurements on an exact figure. Rotate a drawing and verify that its category memberships persist. Allow all classes whose definitions the figure satisfies.

**Parent bridge.** After the triangles, relate the right-angle mark to a quarter-turn at that vertex, even when neither side is horizontal.

## G61. Testing reflection symmetry

Concepts `symmetry`, `shape`. [G03](kindergarten.md#g03-naming-shapes-and-locating-them-in-space) supplies figures and [G07](kindergarten.md#g07-building-figures-and-composing-a-new-shape) helps compare parts. A line of symmetry is a reflection axis that takes the entire figure onto itself. Corresponding points lie at equal perpendicular distances on opposite sides, or on the axis.

| Standard | Grade expectation | Selected evidence |
|---|---|---|
| [4.G.A.3](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=32) | Identify reflection symmetry, recognize figures having it, and draw lines of symmetry. | Fold or reflect a proposed axis. Check that the complete figure matches, including its boundary. |

**Examples.** Identify figures with and without line symmetry and draw all axes in a chosen set. Compare the horizontal and vertical midlines of a 2-by-4 rectangle with a proposed diagonal. Include a square and a scalene triangle.

**Independent check.** MP3. Reflect vertices and edges or fold to test complete coincidence. Confirm two axes for the non-square rectangle, four for the square and none for the scalene triangle. The diagonal can split area equally without passing the reflection test.

**Parent bridge.** After the non-square rectangle, compare an equal-area cut with an actual fold that matches every boundary point.
