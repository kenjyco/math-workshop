## G08. Making ten, finding a missing addend and testing equality

Concepts `laws`, `add`, `equal`, `count`. Use [G05](kindergarten.md#g05-joining-separating-and-splitting-a-whole) for parts and totals and [G06](kindergarten.md#g06-making-ten-and-building-teen-numbers) for complements of ten. An addend contributes to the sum. An equation says its sides have the same value. An unknown can stand in any position.

| Standard | Grade expectation | Selected evidence |
|---|---|---|
| [1.OA.B.3](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=15) | Use properties of operations to add or subtract. | Exchange addends or regroup to make ten. Verify equality of totals and retain subtraction order. |
| [1.OA.B.4](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=15) | Interpret subtraction as finding a missing addend. | Connect 13-8 to 8+□=13. Substitute the result. |
| [1.OA.C.5](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=15) | Relate counting to addition and subtraction. | Start from a known quantity and count changes forward or back. Check the starting number is not counted as a move. |
| [1.OA.C.6](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=15) | Calculate within 20 and attain fluency within 10, using strategies such as making ten, known sums, decomposition, and inverses. | Include a ten-crossing sum and difference, then varied within-ten facts. Check the intermediate split as well as the answer. |
| [1.OA.D.7](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=15) | Interpret equality and decide whether additive equations are true. | Give true and false cases with expressions on either side. Evaluate both sides. |
| [1.OA.D.8](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=15) | Find an unknown in an addition or subtraction equation relating three whole numbers. | Vary unknown position, including the starting quantity in subtraction. Verify by replacement. |

**Examples.** Connect counting on or back, regrouping addends and using inverse relationships. Include 8 + 6 and 13 − 8, and compare a true and a false equation with operations on both sides. Use a box for the unknown, including a starting quantity in subtraction. Formal property names are optional.

**Independent check.** MP7. Count the moves from the start, check each intermediate equality and substitute the recovered unknown into the original equation. Vary facts within ten for fluency and include calculations above ten through twenty.

**Parent bridge.** After making ten, identify exactly which counters moved and why 10 + 4 still describes the original 8 + 6.

## G09. Finding the unknown in an additive story

Concepts `add`, `model`, `equal`. [G05](kindergarten.md#g05-joining-separating-and-splitting-a-whole) supplies joining and separating. [G08](grade-1.md#g08-making-ten-finding-a-missing-addend-and-testing-equality) supplies equality and unknowns. Distinguish the starting amount, change, result, part, whole and difference.

| Standard | Grade expectation | Selected evidence |
|---|---|---|
| [1.OA.A.1](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=15) | Solve additive stories within 20 involving increase, decrease, parts and whole, or comparison, with the unknown in all applicable positions. | Vary which quantity is missing. Check that the drawing and unknown refer to the requested quantity. |
| [1.OA.A.2](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=15) | Solve stories combining three whole numbers with total at most 20. | Draw three collections and record the sum. Account for each collection once. |

**Examples.** Within 20, cover add-to and take-from with result, change and start unknown. For putting together and taking apart, vary total unknown, addend unknown and both addends unknown. For comparison, vary difference, larger and smaller unknown. Include more-than and fewer-than wording. Use objects or a drawing and an equation with a symbol. Add a story with three addends.

**Independent check.** MP4. Have a reviewer identify the unknown from the words before inspecting the equation. Substitute the answer, or rejoin both chosen parts when several splits work. Account for all three quantities in the three-addend total.

**Parent bridge.** After a comparison, mark the unmatched part in a paired drawing and connect its length to the difference.

## G10. Reading tens and ones and counting through 120

Concepts `count`, `place`, `exchange`, `order`. Use [G01](kindergarten.md#g01-counting-a-sequence-and-counting-a-collection) for numeral and quantity, [G06](kindergarten.md#g06-making-ten-and-building-teen-numbers) for ten individual ones, and [G02](kindergarten.md#g02-comparing-collections-and-sorting-by-count) for comparisons. A digit gives the count of units in its position. A bundle of ten ones is one ten.

| Standard | Grade expectation | Selected evidence |
|---|---|---|
| [1.NBT.A.1](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=15) | Count to 120 from any starting number below 120, and read, write, and match numerals to quantities in range. | Cross 99 to 100, then represent a quantity above 100. Check spoken, written, and material records. |
| [1.NBT.B.2](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=15) | Interpret both digits of a two-digit number as tens and ones. | Read 42 as four tens and two ones, then rebuild and count its value. |
| [1.NBT.B.2.a](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=15) | Treat a group of ten ones as one ten. | Bundle and unbundle ten sticks. Check quantity conservation. |
| [1.NBT.B.2.b](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=15) | Recognize 11–19 as one ten plus additional ones. | Compare 14 and 17 with the ten held fixed. Verify the number of extras. |
| [1.NBT.B.2.c](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=15) | Interpret 10 through 90 by tens as groups of tens with zero ones. | Build 60. Match six bundles to the tens digit and the empty ones place to zero. |
| [1.NBT.B.3](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=16) | Compare two two-digit numbers by tens and ones and record >, =, <. | Include equal tens, different tens, and equality. Read each comparison aloud. |

**Examples.** Bundle, unbundle, write and compare two-digit numbers. Use all exact tens from 10 to 90, teens, equal tens with different ones, and equality. Extend the counting and numeral-to-objects task across 99 and up to 120.

**Independent check.** MP6. Independently reconstruct each two-digit comparison from bundles. Exchange one bundle back to ten loose ones without changing the count. Verify that 119 has successor 120 even though the tens-and-ones comparison standard concerns two-digit numerals.

**Parent bridge.** After the exact tens, explain how the zero records an empty ones place.

## G11. Adding and subtracting in tens and ones

Concepts `place`, `exchange`, `add`. [G10](grade-1.md#g10-reading-tens-and-ones-and-counting-through-120) gives tens and ones. [G08](grade-1.md#g08-making-ten-finding-a-missing-addend-and-testing-equality) gives additive reasoning. An exchange renames ten ones as a ten of equal value. A partial sum records a subtotal.

| Standard | Grade expectation | Selected evidence |
|---|---|---|
| [1.NBT.C.4](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=16) | Add within 100, including two-digit plus one-digit and two-digit plus a multiple of ten. Use a model or drawing, explain the strategy, and connect it to writing. | Show both operand forms and a sum that composes a ten. Match like units and written subtotals. |
| [1.NBT.C.5](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=16) | Mentally find ten more or ten less than a two-digit number and explain. | Change the number of tens. Verify by an independent addition or subtraction. |
| [1.NBT.C.6](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=16) | Subtract multiples of ten, both operands 10–90, with zero or positive difference. Model, explain, and connect to writing. | Subtract bundles, including equal operands. Check tens remaining and the written result. |

**Examples.** Link each model to a written calculation and an explanation. Include two-digit plus one-digit, plus a multiple of ten, and plus another two-digit number with a trade. Show mental ten more/less without counting on by ones. For subtraction of exact tens use only 10–90 operands and nonnegative results.

**Independent check.** MP7. Record the ones subtotal before and after a trade. Rebuild the sum using bundles. For a mental change of ten, undo it. For equal multiples of ten subtracted, confirm that no bundles remain.

**Parent bridge.** After the trade, connect the new bundle to the extra 1 recorded in the tens column in a familiar written sum.

## G12. Comparing length indirectly and measuring by iteration

Concepts `length`, `order`. Use the common endpoint from [G04](kindergarten.md#g04-comparing-measurable-attributes-directly). A length unit is a span that can be repeated. A third object can carry a comparison between objects that cannot be placed together.

| Standard | Grade expectation | Selected evidence |
|---|---|---|
| [1.MD.A.1](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=16) | Order three lengths and use a third object for an indirect comparison. | Transfer each length to a strip from a common endpoint. Verify the inferred order. |
| [1.MD.A.2](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=16) | Measure with repeated equal units placed end to end. Use exact whole-unit spans. | Cover a strip with equal tiles. Check both endpoints, gaps, overlap, and tile count. |

**Examples.** Order three objects and compare two by transferring both spans to the same strip. Cover a different strip by an exact whole number of equal units. Include a gapped or overlapping covering for comparison.

**Independent check.** MP3. Rebuild the ordering from the two third-object comparisons. Check both endpoints and the absence of gaps and overlaps, then compare the endpoint spans of two five-tile runs. A gap increases the span between the outer ends without increasing the length covered by tiles. Formal transitivity vocabulary is optional.

**Parent bridge.** After the covering, relate touching unit ends to the intervals that will be printed on a ruler.

## G13. Reading hours and half-hours

Concepts `time`. Begin with the clock face and display. The minute hand tracks progress around an hour. The hour hand continues moving between numerals.

| Standard | Grade expectation | Selected evidence |
|---|---|---|
| [1.MD.B.3](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=16) | Read and write hours and half-hours on analog and digital clocks. | Match 3:00 and 3:30 to faces. Check both hands, including the hour hand halfway to four. |

**Examples.** Read and write each time in both directions between an analog face and a digital display. Pair an exact hour with its half-hour, including the position halfway between 12 and 1.

**Independent check.** MP6. Cover the digital label and have a reviewer recover it from both hands. Reverse the task and check that the shorter hand advances half the distance between adjacent hour marks at the half-hour.

**Parent bridge.** After a matched pair, relate the minute hand's half-turn to the 30 in the display.

## G14. Answering questions from category displays

Concepts `data`, `add`. [G02](kindergarten.md#g02-comparing-collections-and-sorting-by-count) supplies sorting and counts. [G08](grade-1.md#g08-making-ten-finding-a-missing-addend-and-testing-equality) supports differences. A category count tells how many observations have a label. The total counts all observations.

| Standard | Grade expectation | Selected evidence |
|---|---|---|
| [1.MD.C.4](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=16) | Organize, display, and interpret up to three categories, including totals, category counts, and differences. | Build a three-column display from individual responses. Reconcile total and compare columns. |

**Examples.** Build a display from responses in up to three categories. Ask how many in all, how many in each, and how many more or fewer in one than another.

**Independent check.** MP2. Reconstruct the list of category labels from the display up to order, then verify the total and pair entries across two categories to check the difference. Include a tie.

**Parent bridge.** After the comparison, connect the unmatched marks to the subtraction equation.

## G15. Defining attributes and composing in two stages

Concepts `shape`, `compose`. [G03](kindergarten.md#g03-naming-shapes-and-locating-them-in-space) supplies figure names and [G07](kindergarten.md#g07-building-figures-and-composing-a-new-shape) supplies composition. A defining attribute must hold for every member of the class. Incidental features can vary.

| Standard | Grade expectation | Selected evidence |
|---|---|---|
| [1.G.A.1](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=16) | Distinguish defining and incidental attributes. Build and draw figures with the defining attributes. | Vary color, size, and rotation. Check a triangle is closed with three straight sides and reject an open look-alike. |
| [1.G.A.2](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=16) | Compose plane pieces or solids and use a composite in a further composition. | Offer rectangles, squares, trapezoids, triangles, half- and quarter-circles, or cubes, right rectangular prisms, right circular cones and cylinders. Verify both rounds of construction. Formal solid names are optional. |

**Examples.** Build and draw triangles with changes in size, color and orientation. Include a near miss. Compose plane pieces and exhibit how the new shape can itself serve as a piece. Include a solid construction as a second editorial example. Use familiar names for solids.

**Independent check.** MP3. Have a reviewer classify a rotated result by properties, identify the failed property of the near miss, then disassemble both levels of a construction to verify its pieces and final boundary.

**Parent bridge.** After the rotated pair, explain why turning the paper leaves a triangle's defining features intact.

## G16. Halves and fourths of circles and rectangles

Concepts `share`. Use [G07](kindergarten.md#g07-building-figures-and-composing-a-new-shape) to reconstruct a whole from pieces. An equal share of a plane region has the same area as each other share. Half of, fourth of and quarter of name shares relative to a whole.

| Standard | Grade expectation | Selected evidence |
|---|---|---|
| [1.G.A.3](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=16) | Divide circles and rectangles into two or four equal shares. Name halves, fourths/quarters, reconstruct the whole, and compare share size. | Halve and quarter equal-size wholes. Check equal areas, the number needed to rebuild each whole, and the smaller size of fourths. |

**Examples.** Partition both circles and rectangles into two and four equal shares. Name each share, describe the whole as two halves or four fourths, and compare half and quarter of the same-sized whole.

**Independent check.** MP6. Verify equal shares by matching or overlay, and reassemble with no gaps or overlaps. Change the size of the whole and identify why the original share-size comparison needs its reference whole.

**Parent bridge.** After the four-part model, connect “quarter of” to “fourth of” on the same piece.
