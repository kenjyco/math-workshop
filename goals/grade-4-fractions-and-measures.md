## G50. Generating equivalent fractions and comparing unlike units

Concepts `equiv`, `fraction`, `order`. Use [G35](grade-3-fractions-and-measurement.md#g35-unit-fractions-in-regions-and-on-a-number-line) for fractional units, [G36](grade-3-fractions-and-measurement.md#g36-equivalent-names-and-fraction-comparisons) for equivalent locations and [G28](grade-3-operations-and-area.md#g28-deriving-facts-from-multiplication-properties) for multiplication. Subdivision by the same positive whole number changes part size and count while preserving the represented quantity.

| Standard | Grade expectation | Selected evidence |
|---|---|---|
| [4.NF.A.1](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=30) | Explain with a visual fraction model why multiplying numerator and denominator by the same positive whole number preserves value. Use this to recognize and generate equivalents. | Subdivide every part equally. Verify more parts of smaller size cover exactly the original amount. |
| [4.NF.A.2](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=30) | Compare fractions with different numerators and denominators for the same whole. Record >, =, < and justify. | Use common units or a benchmark such as one half. Verify the conclusion against aligned wholes. |

**Examples.** Explain equivalence with a visual fraction model. Compare fractions with different numerators and denominators on the same whole, recording >, < or = and justifying each decision. Include recognition of an already equivalent pair and generation of a new equivalent name. Use common units and a benchmark such as one half in separate comparisons.

**Independent check.** MP3. A reviewer should recover both labels from the same shaded amount and check that every original part was subdivided equally. Test 3/8 against 2/3 using one half, then check by a second same-whole drawing. Stay in the grade 4 denominator set.

**Parent bridge.** After the subdivision, connect the extra boundaries in each part to multiplying both numerator and denominator by the same number.

## G51. Adding fractional units and exchanging a whole in mixed numbers

Concepts `fraction`, `frac-add`, `equal`, `equiv`, `model`. [G35](grade-3-fractions-and-measurement.md#g35-unit-fractions-in-regions-and-on-a-number-line) supplies unit fractions, [G05](kindergarten.md#g05-joining-separating-and-splitting-a-whole) joining and separating and [G36](grade-3-fractions-and-measurement.md#g36-equivalent-names-and-fraction-comparisons) equivalent names. A mixed number combines a whole number and a fractional part. With like denominators each piece has the same size.

| Standard | Grade expectation | Selected evidence |
|---|---|---|
| [4.NF.B.3](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=30) | Understand a/b, for a > 1, as the sum of *a* copies of 1/*b*. | Build 5/4 from five fourths. Keep the unit whole fixed beyond one. |
| [4.NF.B.3.a](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=30) | Interpret fraction addition/subtraction as joining/separating amounts that refer to the same whole. | Join or remove equal-unit pieces. Check the reference whole before computing. |
| [4.NF.B.3.b](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=30) | Decompose a fraction in more than one way with a common denominator, record equations, and justify. | Split 5/8 into 1/8+4/8 and 2/8+3/8. Count eighths in each. |
| [4.NF.B.3.c](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=30) | Add and subtract mixed numbers with like denominators. | Include a subtraction requiring exchange of one whole. Recompose the mixed-number answer. |
| [4.NF.B.3.d](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=30) | Solve like-denominator fraction addition/subtraction stories with a common whole. | Represent two lengths using the same unit length and write an equation. Check the answer in context. |

**Examples.** Build an amount above one from unit fractions. Require more than one decomposition of 5/8, with equations and justification. Include addition and subtraction of like-denominator mixed numbers and stories for both operations on the same whole. Include a difference requiring exchange of a whole.

**Independent check.** MP7. Verify each decomposition by counting unit fractions. For 2 1/4 − 1 3/4 check the exchange into fourths preserves the starting value. Add the difference back. Test denominator and reference whole before combining any pieces.

**Parent bridge.** After the mixed subtraction, compare exchanging one whole for four fourths with exchanging a ten for ten ones in a different unit system.

## G52. Multiplying a fractional amount by a whole-number count

Concepts `frac-mul`, `fraction`, `product`, `model`. [G35](grade-3-fractions-and-measurement.md#g35-unit-fractions-in-regions-and-on-a-number-line) supplies unit fractions and [G27](grade-3-operations-and-area.md#g27-products-both-meanings-of-division-and-missing-factors) supplies equal groups. [G51](grade-4-fractions-and-measures.md#g51-adding-fractional-units-and-exchanging-a-whole-in-mixed-numbers) offers a repeated-sum check. A multiple of a fraction counts copies of that amount.

| Standard | Grade expectation | Selected evidence |
|---|---|---|
| [4.NF.B.4](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=30) | Extend multiplication to a fraction times a whole number. | Show equal copies of a fractional amount and connect the count to a product. |
| [4.NF.B.4.a](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=30) | Interpret a/b as *a* copies of 1/*b*. | Model 5/4=5 × (1/4). Verify the four parts per whole and five taken. |
| [4.NF.B.4.b](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=30) | Express a whole-number multiple of a/b in unit fractions and calculate. | 3 × (2/5)=6/5. Check six fifths against three equal pairs. |
| [4.NF.B.4.c](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=30) | Solve stories multiplying a fraction by a whole number. | Use equal fractional measures per person. Give total, unit, and a whole-number size check. |

**Examples.** Use 5/4 as five copies of 1/4, then use a visual model and equation for three copies of 2/5. Include a story with a fractional measure per person and a whole-number count. Bound the total between whole numbers.

**Independent check.** MP2. Independently count unit pieces two ways, by group and across the whole model. Check the numerator of the product, denominator, original unit and size bound. Restrict the multiplier to whole numbers.

**Parent bridge.** After the model, identify the number of groups and what one group measures before reading the multiplication sentence.

## G53. Renaming tenths and hundredths and comparing decimals

Concepts `equiv`, `frac-add`, `decimal`, `fraction`, `order`. Use [G41](grade-4-whole-numbers.md#g41-reading-comparing-and-rounding-through-one-million) for place, [G35](grade-3-fractions-and-measurement.md#g35-unit-fractions-in-regions-and-on-a-number-line) for fractional units, [G50](grade-4-fractions-and-measures.md#g50-generating-equivalent-fractions-and-comparing-unlike-units) for equivalent names and [G51](grade-4-fractions-and-measures.md#g51-adding-fractional-units-and-exchanging-a-whole-in-mixed-numbers) for adding like units. A tenth contains ten hundredths. The decimal point fixes the ones place.

| Standard | Grade expectation | Selected evidence |
|---|---|---|
| [4.NF.C.5](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=31) | Rename tenths as hundredths and add a tenths fraction to a hundredths fraction. | Use a common hundred-grid. Verify the unit conversion precedes addition. |
| [4.NF.C.6](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=31) | Write fractions with denominator 10 or 100 in decimal notation. | Connect a grid, fraction, decimal, and point on a line. Include an empty tenths position. |
| [4.NF.C.7](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=31) | Compare decimals through hundredths for the same whole. Record >, =, < and justify. | Include 0.7 = 0.70 and 0.7 > 0.68. Check with hundredths or an aligned scale. |

**Examples.** On a hundred-grid rename 3/10 and add 7/100. Link grid, fraction, decimal and number-line location. Include an empty tenths place, a trailing zero, equality, and two different decimals whose lengths as strings might mislead.

**Independent check.** MP6. A reviewer should independently count cells or locate lengths on a common unit line, justify >, < and =, and reject comparisons made on inconsistent wholes. Use tenths and hundredths for the addition connection. General unlike-denominator addition is beyond scope.

**Parent bridge.** After the grid, relate 0.7 and 0.70 to the same covered region, and contrast 0.07 by its place size.

## G54. Converting units in the same measurement system

Concepts `units`, `product`. [G38](grade-3-fractions-and-measurement.md#g38-measuring-mass-and-liquid-volume-and-operating-in-one-unit) supplies measuring units, [G37](grade-3-fractions-and-measurement.md#g37-measuring-elapsed-minutes-across-an-hour) supplies time units, and [G27](grade-3-operations-and-area.md#g27-products-both-meanings-of-division-and-missing-factors) supports conversion by equal groups. A conversion factor counts smaller units in one larger unit.

| Standard | Grade expectation | Selected evidence |
|---|---|---|
| [4.MD.A.1](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=31) | Know relative unit sizes, convert larger to smaller within one system, and record equivalents in a two-column table. Cover km/m/cm, kg/g, lb/oz, L/mL, and hr/min/sec. Feet/inches are a source example. | Label both columns and the conversion factor. Verify successive rows and same-system quantities. |

**Examples.** Give two-column equivalence tables for length (km, m, cm), mass (kg, g), weight (lb, oz), liquid volume (L, mL) and time (hr, min, sec). Include feet to inches as a familiar example. State relative sizes and express larger units in smaller units within one system.

**Independent check.** MP8. Independently recover every table row from the one-unit row. Check that doubling the larger-unit count doubles the smaller-unit count. Keep quantity fixed and unit symbols explicit. Avoid comparisons between mass and weight systems.

**Parent bridge.** After one table, show why the numerical measure increases when the unit becomes smaller.

## G55. Solving measurement stories with scaled diagrams

Concepts `units`, `scale`, `model`. Use [G23](grade-2.md#g23-representing-length-arithmetic-on-a-number-line) for measurement scales, [G37](grade-3-fractions-and-measurement.md#g37-measuring-elapsed-minutes-across-an-hour) for durations, [G25](grade-2.md#g25-counting-money-and-solving-purchase-stories) for money, [G53](grade-4-fractions-and-measures.md#g53-renaming-tenths-and-hundredths-and-comparing-decimals) for decimals and [G54](grade-4-fractions-and-measures.md#g54-converting-units-in-the-same-measurement-system) for conversions. An interval on a scale has a specified unit and a measured span.

| Standard | Grade expectation | Selected evidence |
|---|---|---|
| [4.MD.A.2](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=31) | Use four operations in problems about distance, time intervals, liquid volume, mass, and money. Include simple fractions/decimals and conversions from larger units to smaller. Represent quantities with diagrams featuring a measurement scale. | Cover the quantity families across examples. Check spacing, conversion, operation, and final unit. |

**Examples.** Across examples use all four operations in contexts of distance, time intervals, liquid volume, mass and money. Include simple fractions and decimals and a larger-to-smaller conversion. Represent quantities with diagrams that have a measurement scale, such as a split trip in kilometers with an answer in meters. Include the units at each change of scale and explain which number the requested answer measures.

**Independent check.** MP4. Independently derive each operation from the units and requested quantity. Read the same lengths or intervals back from the scale, verify each conversion and substitute the answer in context. Keep simple fraction and decimal cases within the named measurement work.

**Parent bridge.** After a conversion story, connect the two scales or unit labels that describe the same span.

## G56. Using fractional measurement data in sums and differences

Concepts `plot`, `frac-add`. [G40](grade-3-fractions-and-measurement.md#g40-measuring-quarter-inches-and-plotting-repeated-lengths) supplies measurement plots, [G50](grade-4-fractions-and-measures.md#g50-generating-equivalent-fractions-and-comparing-unlike-units) equivalent names and [G51](grade-4-fractions-and-measures.md#g51-adding-fractional-units-and-exchanging-a-whole-in-mixed-numbers) like-unit operations. Each mark represents one original observation. Repeated values retain their multiplicity.

| Standard | Grade expectation | Selected evidence |
|---|---|---|
| [4.MD.B.4](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=31) | Display measurement data in halves, quarters, and eighths on line plots. Use the displayed data for fraction sums/differences. | Include repeated measurements. Reconcile marks to observations and read both operands from the plot. |

**Examples.** Plot ribbon lengths 3/8, 1/2, 1/2 and 3/4 yard on an eighth-yard scale. Use data displayed in halves, fourths and eighths to answer both an addition and a subtraction question, including the longest-minus-shortest span.

**Independent check.** MP6. Independently recover four ribbons and both observations at 4/8. Read the operands for each question from the plot and recompute in eighths. Confirm the difference is 3/8 yard and that an observation is used only as many times as the question allows.

**Parent bridge.** After the plot, show why one half and four eighths share a position while two half-yard ribbons still need two marks.
