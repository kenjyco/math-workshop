## G27. Products, both meanings of division and missing factors

Concepts `groups`, `product`, `divide`, `model`, `equal`. Use [G20](grade-2.md#g20-pairs-equal-addends-and-square-arrays) for equal groups and [G08](grade-1.md#g08-making-ten-finding-a-missing-addend-and-testing-equality) for an unknown in an equation. A factor can count groups or units per group. A quotient can count the groups formed or the amount in each group.

| Standard | Grade expectation | Selected evidence |
|---|---|---|
| [3.OA.A.1](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=23) | Interpret a whole-number product in context. | Describe four bags with six objects each. Label group count, size, and total. |
| [3.OA.A.2](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=23) | Interpret a whole-number quotient both as group size and as number of groups. | Use 24 objects in two stories. Check that the answer unit changes with the question. |
| [3.OA.A.3](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=23) | Solve multiplication/division problems within 100 involving equal groups, arrays, and measurement quantities. | Cover unknown total, group size, and group count across Table 2 settings. Match picture and equation to the unknown. |
| [3.OA.A.4](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=23) | Find an unknown whole number in a three-quantity multiplication or division equation. | Put the unknown in different positions. Substitute and check. |
| [3.OA.B.6](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=23) | Interpret division through a missing factor. | Solve 42÷6 via 6 × □=42. Multiply back. |

**Examples.** Use 24 objects shared among six recipients, then 24 objects packed six per group. Give each result its unit. Across equal-group, array and measurement situations in Table 2, vary unknown total, group size and group count. Show equations with the unknown in different positions including the divisor.

**Independent check.** MP2. Recover each original total by multiplication. Independently match each operand to the drawing and story, including lengths representing repeated units. For within-100 problems the product or dividend is at most 100 and the divisor is nonzero.

**Parent bridge.** After the paired stories, ask what four counts in each one and connect both readings of 24 ÷ 6 to the same missing-factor equation.

## G28. Deriving facts from multiplication properties

Concepts `laws`, `product`, `divide`. [G27](grade-3-operations-and-area.md#g27-products-both-meanings-of-division-and-missing-factors) supplies factors, products and inverse division. Switching factors, regrouping three factors and distributing over a split each preserve the whole product. These are the commutative, associative and distributive properties. Formal names are optional.

| Standard | Grade expectation | Selected evidence |
|---|---|---|
| [3.OA.B.5](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=23) | Use operation properties in multiplication and division strategies. Formal property names are optional. | Rotate an array, regroup factors, and split a factor. Verify each transformation and preserve division order. |
| [3.OA.C.7](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=23) | Multiply and divide fluently within 100. By year end recall products of every pair of one-digit numbers. | Pair a fact with inverse divisions where the divisor is nonzero. Verify recall scope independently of a 2–12 reference chart. |

**Examples.** Rotate an array, regroup three factors, and split an unfamiliar fact into known ones. Use a related multiplication to derive a division. Include zero as a factor and dividend where defined, and one as a factor or divisor.

**Independent check.** MP7. Independently count array entries or reassemble the split. Check inverse divisions by multiplication. Plan the whole year-end recall set of products of pairs of one-digit numbers, alongside fluent multiplication and division with product or dividend within 100. Keep zero divisors out of inverse families.

**Parent bridge.** After the split array, connect its pieces to two known products from which a larger fact can be recovered.

## G29. Explaining patterns in tables and products of tens

Concepts `pattern`, `laws`, `place`, `product`. Use [G20](grade-2.md#g20-pairs-equal-addends-and-square-arrays) for parity, [G28](grade-3-operations-and-area.md#g28-deriving-facts-from-multiplication-properties) for properties and [G18](grade-2.md#g18-making-hundreds-counting-in-increments-and-comparing) for tens. A multiple of ten counts tens. A table entry inherits a relationship from its row and column.

| Standard | Grade expectation | Selected evidence |
|---|---|---|
| [3.OA.D.9](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=23) | Identify arithmetic patterns, including in operation tables, and explain using properties. | Show why a row of multiples of four is even using two equal addends. Check the explanation extends beyond the displayed cells. |
| [3.NBT.A.3](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=24) | Multiply a one-digit whole number by a multiple of ten from 10–90 using place value and properties. | Read 6 × 40 as 24 tens. Convert to 240 ones. |

**Examples.** Identify patterns in addition and multiplication tables and explain them using properties. Include why every multiple of four is even. For one digit times an exact ten from 10–90, build equal groups of tens and convert the product back to ones.

**Independent check.** MP8. Extend a table by an unseen entry and justify the prediction for any entry in that row. For 6 × 40 recover the answer by adding six groups of forty and check the unit of the intermediate 24.

**Parent bridge.** After the tens product, connect the zero in the written result to the count of tens and the empty ones place.

## G30. Computing and estimating with whole-number place value

Concepts `round`, `place`, `exchange`, `add`. [G18](grade-2.md#g18-making-hundreds-counting-in-increments-and-comparing) supplies ordered places, [G19](grade-2.md#g19-adding-partial-sums-and-exchanging-across-a-zero) supplies equal-value trades, and [G23](grade-2.md#g23-representing-length-arithmetic-on-a-number-line) supplies intervals. Nearest ten or hundred means the closer of adjacent multiples of that unit. A midpoint needs the usual round-up convention.

| Standard | Grade expectation | Selected evidence |
|---|---|---|
| [3.NBT.A.1](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=24) | Round whole numbers to the nearest ten or hundred using place value. | Mark neighboring multiples, midpoint, exact multiples, and tie cases. Verify distances and conventional whole-number rounding. |
| [3.NBT.A.2](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=24) | Fluently add and subtract within 1,000 using valid place-value, property, or inverse-based strategies or algorithms. | Compare partial sums with another written method. Verify each intermediate value and inverse. |

**Examples.** Locate values just below, at and just above a midpoint, as well as an exact multiple, for tens and hundreds. Pair an estimate with fluent exact addition and subtraction within 1,000. Show partial sums and an alternative strategy or algorithm.

**Independent check.** MP6. Independently compare distances on the line for the rounded values, including a carry across a hundred. Verify exact sums and differences by the inverse operation. Label estimated and exact values distinctly.

**Parent bridge.** After the paired calculations, explain how a rounded check catches a misplaced digit even when it cannot determine the exact answer.

## G31. Representing two-step whole-number problems

Concepts `model`, `equal`, `round`. Use [G17](grade-2.md#g17-mental-facts-and-one--and-two-step-additive-stories) for additive stories, [G27](grade-3-operations-and-area.md#g27-products-both-meanings-of-division-and-missing-factors) for multiplicative situations, [G08](grade-1.md#g08-making-ten-finding-a-missing-addend-and-testing-equality) for equations and [G30](grade-3-operations-and-area.md#g30-computing-and-estimating-with-whole-number-place-value) for rounding. A letter names one unknown quantity, with its unit.

| Standard | Grade expectation | Selected evidence |
|---|---|---|
| [3.OA.D.8](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=23) | Solve two-step whole-number stories with whole-number answers across the four operations. Use a letter, assess reasonableness mentally or by estimation including rounding, and observe conventional order without parentheses. | Distinguish a subtotal from the requested total. Check the equation, operation order, estimate, and exact answer. |

**Examples.** Cover all four operations across two-step stories with whole-number inputs and answers. Include a case such as 30 − 3 × 4 and a case where a sum must be found before multiplying. Demonstrate conventional order for expressions without parentheses and the meaning of any grouping used.

**Independent check.** MP1 and MP4. Have a reviewer label each quantity from the story, independently solve in two stages, then substitute into the equation. Assess reasonableness with mental calculation and estimates including rounding. Show a correct subtotal paired with the question it actually answers.

**Parent bridge.** After the two-stage representation, distinguish the amount found at the first step from the amount represented by the final letter.

## G32. Measuring with square units and adding rectangle areas

Concepts `area`, `length`, `units`, `product`, `add`, `groups`, `laws`, `compose`. [G20](grade-2.md#g20-pairs-equal-addends-and-square-arrays) supplies rows of equal squares, [G27](grade-3-operations-and-area.md#g27-products-both-meanings-of-division-and-missing-factors) supplies products, [G07](kindergarten.md#g07-building-figures-and-composing-a-new-shape) supplies composition. Area measures surface covered. A unit square has side one length unit. Rectilinear boundaries follow perpendicular directions.

| Standard | Grade expectation | Selected evidence |
|---|---|---|
| [3.MD.C.5](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=25) | Understand area as a measurable property of a plane figure. | Use a region, its boundary, and a covering. Identify which part the area measures. |
| [3.MD.C.5.a](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=25) | A square one length unit on each side has area one square unit. | Label a 1 cm square. Verify side units and area unit separately. |
| [3.MD.C.5.b](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=25) | A region tiled exactly by n unit squares has area n square units. | Audit a covering for gaps, overlaps, and counted squares. |
| [3.MD.C.6](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=25) | Measure by counting unit squares, including square cm, m, in, ft, and improvised square units. | Label the chosen tile and count. Check the named square unit matches the tile. |
| [3.MD.C.7](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=25) | Relate area to multiplication and addition. | Show the same region as a tiled rectangle and as a sum of subregions. |
| [3.MD.C.7.a](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=25) | Tile a whole-number-sided rectangle and show that multiplying its side lengths gives the same area. | Label rows and squares per row. Reconcile count and product. |
| [3.MD.C.7.b](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=25) | Use side products in real and mathematical rectangle-area problems, and use rectangular areas to represent whole-number products. | Work once from lengths to area and once from a product to a rectangle. Check units and factors. |
| [3.MD.C.7.c](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=25) | Use a tiled rectangle split across one side to demonstrate distribution over a sum. | Color or label two non-overlapping regions. Verify their dimensions and sum against the whole. |
| [3.MD.C.7.d](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=25) | Decompose rectilinear regions into non-overlapping rectangles and add areas, including in applied problems. | Split an L-shaped floor. Verify the pieces cover exactly the floor and compare with a second split. |

**Examples.** Start with exact tiling, then a side product, a one-side split showing distribution, and a rectilinear floor with two possible rectangle partitions. Include real and purely mathematical rectangles and represent a given product by a rectangular area. Tile with named and improvised square units across the set.

**Independent check.** MP7. Recount the 3-by-4 rectangle directly. Attach a 2-by-2 square along a two-unit outside segment, with disjoint interiors, and check 16 square units by an independent split. Verify full coverage, units and absence of double-counted cells at seams.

**Parent bridge.** After the split, connect each term in the sum to the complete rows and columns of its own region.

## G33. Tracing perimeter and comparing it with area

Concepts `perimeter`, `area`. [G22](grade-2.md#g22-selecting-a-length-tool-estimating-and-measuring) supplies lengths and [G32](grade-3-operations-and-area.md#g32-measuring-with-square-units-and-adding-rectangle-areas) supplies area. Perimeter is the total boundary length of a closed figure. A missing side can be recovered from the other sides and the perimeter.

| Standard | Grade expectation | Selected evidence |
|---|---|---|
| [3.MD.D.8](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=25) | Solve polygon-perimeter problems, including a missing side. Exhibit rectangles with equal perimeter/different area and equal area/different perimeter. | Trace the boundary, solve a missing length, and provide both kinds of rectangle pair. Recalculate all measures. |

**Examples.** Include a polygon in context, a mathematical figure with a missing length, and rectangles 3 by 4, 2 by 5 and 2 by 6. Compare equal perimeters with different areas and equal areas with different perimeters.

**Independent check.** MP3. Independently walk each boundary once and count square units inside. Validate both counterexample pairs and restore the missing length to the perimeter sum. Use linear units around and square units inside.

**Parent bridge.** After the two comparisons, show on the same drawing which measure a fence and which measure a covering would use.

## G34. Classifying quadrilaterals by shared attributes

Concepts `shape`. [G15](grade-1.md#g15-defining-attributes-and-composing-in-two-stages) and [G21](grade-2.md#g21-classifying-figures-and-checking-equal-shares) supply defining attributes and polygon names. A quadrilateral has four straight sides. A rectangle has four right angles. A rhombus has four equal sides. A square satisfies both descriptions.

| Standard | Grade expectation | Selected evidence |
|---|---|---|
| [3.G.A.1](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=26) | Classify by shared attributes. Recognize rhombuses, rectangles, and squares as quadrilaterals and draw a quadrilateral outside those subcategories. | Use overlapping categories and a four-sided counterexample. Verify side and angle claims. |

**Examples.** Show rhombuses, rectangles and squares within quadrilaterals. Draw a simple irregular quadrilateral with neither four right angles nor four equal sides. Include a rotated square and a non-square rhombus.

**Independent check.** MP3. Test each classification from the properties, allowing a figure to belong to several categories. Independently verify that the outside example still has four sides and fails the narrower definitions.

**Parent bridge.** After the square, connect its familiar name to the other two names its properties justify.
