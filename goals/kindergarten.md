# Kindergarten

Use whole-number quantities. Kindergarten drawings can be simple marks showing a relationship. Objects and drawings can record work where the standard offers a choice. Writing equations is encouraged. [CCSS Mathematics, grade section](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=11)

## G01. Counting a sequence and counting a collection

Concepts `count`, `card`. Begin directly with ordered words and small collections. A numeral records a number. Cardinality is the size of a set, including the empty set.

| Standard | Grade expectation | Selected evidence |
|---|---|---|
| [K.CC.A.1](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=11) | Count to 100 by ones and by tens. | Two spoken or indicated sequences. Check decade transitions and the endpoint. |
| [K.CC.A.2](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=11) | Continue from a supplied number within the familiar sequence. | Start at 37. Check the next number and passage through 40. |
| [K.CC.A.3](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=11) | Write 0–20 and use these numerals for set sizes, including the empty set. | Match empty and occupied mats to written numerals. Check 0, teen numerals, and 20. |
| [K.CC.B.4](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=11) | Connect the counting process to how many objects there are. | One collection used across the next three checks. |
| [K.CC.B.4.a](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=11) | Pair each object once with one number word in sequence. | Track touched objects. Verify no omission, repeat, or skipped word. |
| [K.CC.B.4.b](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=11) | The last word gives the total, invariant under rearrangement or counting order. | Spread and reorder the same counters. Verify object membership and total. |
| [K.CC.B.4.c](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=11) | Each successive number means one more object. | Add one counter after counting. Check the new total against the successor. |
| [K.CC.B.5](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=11) | Count up to 20 objects in a line, rectangular array, or circle, up to 10 scattered, and produce a requested set of 1–20. | Include all four layouts and a count-out task. Mark a starting object on the circle and stop at the requested quantity. |

**Examples.** Connect a spoken sequence to a written quantity. Include a full run to 100 in both increments, a count starting partway through a decade, and objects matched to the numerals 0–20. Use a marked starting object on the circle. Distinguish finding the total from stopping when a requested total is reached.

**Independent check.** MP1. Have a reviewer trace the chosen route around the circle and then recount in the opposite direction. Each object must have exactly one partner word in either route. Add a new object and check that the last word advances once.

**Parent bridge.** After the rearranged set, explain why spreading seven counters changes the space occupied while preserving how many there are.

## G02. Comparing collections and sorting by count

Concepts `order`, `card`, `data`. Use [G01](kindergarten.md#g01-counting-a-sequence-and-counting-a-collection) for one-to-one counting and numeral meanings. A category groups items by a stated attribute. Greater, fewer and equal compare counts.

| Standard | Grade expectation | Selected evidence |
|---|---|---|
| [K.CC.C.6](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=11) | Compare two collections of up to ten objects each. | Pair across sets, including a tie and a widely spaced smaller set. Verify leftovers. |
| [K.CC.C.7](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=11) | Compare two written numerals from 1–10. | Compare numeral cards without needing to see the collections. Check both orders and equality. |
| [K.MD.B.3](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=12) | Classify by given categories, count each, then order the categories by count. Each category has at most ten objects. | Sort a mixed handful, label groups, and order their totals. Reconcile the counts to the handful. |

**Examples.** Compare by matching before using numerals alone. Sort objects by a supplied rule and order the resulting categories by frequency. Include tied categories and a spread-out group that looks larger.

**Independent check.** MP3. Pair items across two groups and use the unmatched items to justify the comparison. Recount the sorted groups against the original collection, then reverse the order of the numeral cards and check the spoken relation reverses for unequal values.

**Parent bridge.** After the matching example, connect “more” to the items left without partners.

## G03. Naming shapes and locating them in space

Concepts `shape`. Begin directly with objects and plane figures. Use corner, side, flat face and curved surface where each applies. Position words require a reference object.

| Standard | Grade expectation | Selected evidence |
|---|---|---|
| [K.G.A.1](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=12) | Name shapes in the surroundings and describe relative positions. | Locate square, circle, triangle, rectangle, hexagon, cube, cone, cylinder, and sphere examples. Use above/below, beside/next to, in front of/behind with a named reference object. |
| [K.G.A.2](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=12) | Recognize shapes across changes of orientation and size. | Turn and resize a triangle. Check its name still applies. |
| [K.G.A.3](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=12) | Distinguish plane shapes and solids. | Match a circle drawing and a sphere model to descriptions. Check whether the referent is a face, outline, or whole solid. |
| [K.G.B.4](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=12) | Compare plane and solid shapes across sizes and orientations using informal attributes. | Describe corners, sides, flat faces, or curved surfaces as appropriate. Check each claim on both objects. |

**Examples.** Name and compare examples in different sizes and orientations, with both plane and solid cases. Describe a cylinder beside a cube and identify a circular face. Include a rotated square and a triangle with no horizontal side.

**Independent check.** MP6. Independently identify which object each position word locates. Test every stated attribute against the actual figure or solid, including a curved surface. Rotation and scaling must preserve the classification.

**Parent bridge.** After the solid and face pair, distinguish naming a can's circular end from naming the whole can.

## G04. Comparing measurable attributes directly

Concepts `length`, `units`, `order`. Begin with two physical objects. Length describes a span and weight describes a different attribute. [G02](kindergarten.md#g02-comparing-collections-and-sorting-by-count) supplies helpful comparison language.

| Standard | Grade expectation | Selected evidence |
|---|---|---|
| [K.MD.A.1](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=12) | Describe more than one measurable attribute of an object. | Describe the length and weight of the same book. Name which attribute each comparison concerns. |
| [K.MD.A.2](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=12) | Directly compare two objects on a shared attribute and describe the difference. | Align two strips at one end or compare two weights on a balance. Check the direction of the comparison. |

**Examples.** Describe the length and weight of one object. Choose objects for which the longer one is lighter, and a second pair equal in one attribute. Specify how to align ends and how to read the two sides of a balance.

**Independent check.** MP5. Ask which comparison the aligned ends can settle and which needs the balance. Reposition both strips at a common endpoint and confirm that changing their position preserves length.

**Parent bridge.** After the pair, put the attribute into the comparison sentence so that “bigger” has a definite meaning.

## G05. Joining, separating and splitting a whole

Concepts `add`, `model`, `equal`. [G01](kindergarten.md#g01-counting-a-sequence-and-counting-a-collection) supplies quantities for the models. Joining or putting together gives a total. Taking away or taking apart relates a whole to its parts.

| Standard | Grade expectation | Selected evidence |
|---|---|---|
| [K.OA.A.1](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=11) | Represent joining or separating quantities. Objects, movement, sounds, images, words, drawings, and notation are possible modes. | Act out a change and draw its before/after quantities. Check the operation matches the action. |
| [K.OA.A.2](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=11) | Solve addition and subtraction stories and calculations within 10. | Joining and removal stories. Recount the result, including a result of zero. |
| [K.OA.A.3](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=11) | Split a number at most 10 into two parts in multiple ways. Record each with a drawing or equation. | Two different splits of seven. Recombine each to recover seven. |
| [K.OA.A.5](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=11) | Add and subtract fluently within 5. | Small related sums and differences in varied order. Verify accuracy and ready use across cases. |

**Examples.** Use small objects, a simple relationship drawing and spoken descriptions for stories and calculations. Include alternative splits of the same whole, a zero part, and a take-all story. Keep calculations within ten and distinguish the smaller fluency range.

**Independent check.** MP2. Have a reviewer reconstruct the start and end collections from the picture and check every split by rejoining. Include related addition and subtraction facts throughout the within-five range. A simple drawing may record a split. Student-written equations are encouraged.

**Parent bridge.** After the split, connect the two parts in the picture to the numbers on either side of an addition equation.

## G06. Making ten and building teen numbers

Concepts `add`, `place`, `card`. [G01](kindergarten.md#g01-counting-a-sequence-and-counting-a-collection) supplies the counted units and [G05](kindergarten.md#g05-joining-separating-and-splitting-a-whole) the part–whole relation. A complement fills a whole ten. In a teen number the ten consists of ten individual ones.

| Standard | Grade expectation | Selected evidence |
|---|---|---|
| [K.OA.A.4](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=11) | For each starting number 1–9, find its partner to make ten and record it with a drawing or equation. | Ten-frame with filled and empty spaces. Check each pair totals ten. |
| [K.NBT.A.1](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=12) | Build and take apart 11–19 as ten individual ones plus 1–9 more. Record the composition or decomposition. | Group ten counters and leave extras. Verify both the ten ones and the total in a drawing or equation. |

**Examples.** Use a ten-frame first to show what is missing, then a full frame and extra counters to show what is present. Record the ten partner for every start from 1 through 9 and the teen construction in drawings or equations.

**Independent check.** MP7. Check seven occupied and three empty cells against ten. Independently recount teen models including 11 and 19 after moving the extra ones. Keep the ten visible as a collection before introducing the grade 1 bundle.

**Parent bridge.** After the frames, compare the questions “How many more to ten?” and “How many beyond ten?”

## G07. Building figures and composing a new shape

Concepts `shape`, `compose`. Use [G03](kindergarten.md#g03-naming-shapes-and-locating-them-in-space) to name the shapes and their parts. To compose is to join parts into a whole. The boundary traces the outside of the result.

| Standard | Grade expectation | Selected evidence |
|---|---|---|
| [K.G.B.5](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=12) | Model real shapes by building from components and by drawing. | Build a stick triangle and draw it. Verify closure and correspondence. |
| [K.G.B.6](https://corestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/ADA-Compliant-Math-Standards.pdf#page=12) | Combine simple shapes into a larger one. | Join two suitable triangles into a rectangle. Trace the new outside boundary. |

**Examples.** Build and draw an everyday triangular shape, then join two fitted triangles edge to edge. Specify which edges must match for the chosen rectangle to close.

**Independent check.** MP7. Trace the outside without retracing the shared edge. Separate the two pieces and reconstruct the original outline, checking for gaps and overlaps.

**Parent bridge.** After the composition, show where a side of each triangle went when the outside became a rectangle.
