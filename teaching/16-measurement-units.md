# Choosing and converting measurement units

**How can a quantity stay fixed while its measurement number changes?**

## Read the instrument before the number

Grade 3 starts with the attribute. Ask someone to say the instrument's unit aloud before you read its numeral. *Mass* tells how much matter is in an object. A gram (g) and a kilogram (kg) are mass units. *Liquid volume* tells how much space a liquid occupies. A liter (L) is a liquid-volume unit. In the [three sample measurements](../examples/grade-3/weighing-and-pouring.md#three-sample-readings), compare a packet with a 50 g mass. The feel of the sack suggests three 1 kg bags, and the jug looks close to three 1 L bottles. Test each estimate on its own instrument. For the packet, it takes another 5 g to level the pans, so the packet measures 55 g. The sack moves the zeroed scale to 3 **kg**. The water surface, read at eye level in the level jug, reaches 3 **L**. The equal numerals answer different questions.

In the [four one-step stories](../examples/grade-3/weighing-and-pouring.md#four-questions-about-measured-amounts), keep the same unit for the whole calculation. Join 55 and 35 grams, then take 35 back from 90 g. Pour two liters out of five and the other three stay. Count four 3 kg sacks to get 12 kg. Share 12 L among three cans by giving each a liter per round. Four rounds fill each to 4 L. These Grade 3 questions use same-unit amounts in grams, kilograms or liters.

## Build a row from one larger unit

Grade 4 converts a larger unit into smaller units in the same measurement system. The *conversion factor* tells how many of the smaller units fit into one larger one. Build a two-column table by labeling the left column in larger units and the right in smaller units. To fill the row for five, multiply both entries in the one-unit row by five. Read the one-unit row of each of the [eight conversion tables](../examples/grade-4/measuring-and-plotting.md#g54--read-across-a-conversion-table). A kilometer contains 1,000 meters, a kilogram 1,000 grams and a liter 1,000 milliliters. In each table, five of the larger units must contain five copies of its first row. A meter contains a hundred centimeters, a foot twelve inches, a pound sixteen ounces. Two pounds give 32 ounces. Take two such pairs and one more pound: 32 + 32 + 16 = 80 ounces for five pounds. For time, an hour contains sixty minutes and each minute sixty seconds. Keep the two unit labels visible even though both factors are sixty.

## What must the answer measure?

Use [equal intervals](05-measuring-length.md) to read the [route to the bridge and gate](../examples/grade-4/trip-scale.svg). Count three half-kilometer intervals to the bridge and two to the gate. A half-kilometer is 500 meters, so five intervals give **2,500 m**. Converting one leg at a time checks it: 1,500 m plus 1,000 m. Neither the physical width of your screen nor the number of upright marks measures the trip.

Follow the [five applications](../examples/grade-4/measuring-and-plotting.md#g55--what-does-the-answer-measure) by saying the requested unit before calculating. A warmup uses eighteen of sixty minutes, leaving 42 minutes. Four bottles of three quarters of a liter total twelve quarters, or 3 L. Eight bags share 2 kg, or 2,000 g. Two hundred fifty **grams per bag** works because four bags hold 1,000 g and eight restore all 2 kg. For a ticket and a map, rename the prices as 70 and 68 cents. Their sum is 138 cents, or $1.38. Removing the ticket price recovers the map price.

The later applications use [equal groups](09-equal-groups.md), [elapsed time](07-clock-readings.md), [fractional lengths](14-fraction-arithmetic.md) and [decimal money amounts](15-cents-and-hundredths.md). To interpret a multistep answer, also distinguish intermediate quantities from the requested one.
