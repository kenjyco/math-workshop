# Why the product has two written rows

**Where does each digit of a multidigit product come from?**

## Trace each row to its part of the rectangle

In the Grade 4 [23-by-14 rectangle](../examples/four-region-multiplication.svg), put a finger on the short width, 3. Follow it down. It meets a height of 10 **and** a height of 4. It must contribute twice, once to each region it crosses. Do the same for the width 20. This *place-value partition* splits each side into its tens and ones. A *partial product* is the product for one pair of parts. In the [area model](../examples/four-region-multiplication.md#area-model-for-23--14), 200 and 30 cover the upper band. The lower band contains 80 and 12. Their shared edges occupy no area. Together those four regions cover the original rectangle once.

Read the [stacked calculation](../examples/four-region-multiplication.md#parent-bridge) from the bottom band first. The 4 in 14 counts four ones, so the *ones row* records four twenty-threes, 92. Point to the 1 before writing the *tens row*. Ask someone which unit that 1 counts. One ten copies the full width **ten times**, giving 23 tens, written 230. Align both rows in ones, tens and hundreds before adding. To [reassemble in a different direction](../examples/grade-4/products-and-quotients.md#g46--reassemble-the-product), add down the columns: 200 + 80 and 30 + 12 give 280 and 42, again 322. For a separate [check by compensation](../examples/four-region-multiplication.md#check-by-compensation), begin with fifteen groups worth 345. Remove the one extra group of 23 to recover 322.

## Keep an empty place in view

For [four copies of 2,036](../examples/grade-4/products-and-quotients.md#four-copies-of-2036), tell what the zero counts before calculating. There are no hundreds in each original copy. The other places contribute 8,000, 120 and 24, which together make 8,144. A hundred appears in the result because the tens combined to make one. Double 2,036 to get 4,072. Double again to check 8,144. The Grade 4 models here cover two digits by two digits or up to four digits by one.

## Where can you safely continue?

Read the [two inconsistent records](../examples/four-region-multiplication.md#two-incorrect-records-for-23--14) and the [other visible scratch work](../examples/grade-4/products-and-quotients.md#read-the-recorded-work-for-23--14) one line at a time. A lower-left cell of 60 would cover only three rows of twenty. The first visible discrepancy is there, even when the final answer says 322. If the four cells are correct and the sum says 312, keep the cells and redo the addition. For a missing or repeated corner, point to one region per term. The missing 12 in 310 or the extra 12 in 334 explains exactly what to restore or remove.

Rows of 92 and 230 with no total are unfinished. Resume by adding aligned columns, then compare the written 322 with the answer field. A copy of 232 changes the value after correct scratch work. If only 312 survives, you can check its value but can't tell whether any particular row or copy went wrong.

[Cutting a tiled floor](12-area-and-perimeter.md) can help you picture the argument. To [use a product to check division](19-quotient-places.md), you'll also track groups already counted and the amount remaining.
