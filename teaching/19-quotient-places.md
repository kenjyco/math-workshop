# What each quotient digit counts

**How can we tell whether a zero belongs in the quotient and whether division is finished?**

## Credit the groups you have actually made

In Grade 4, try covering the group-count column in the [two paths through 84 divided by 4](../examples/grade-4/products-and-quotients.md#g47--count-groups-as-amounts-come-off). The *dividend* is the amount you start with. The *divisor* here gives the size of one group. If forty items came off, how many groups did you just make? Ten groups of four use forty items. Ten is a *partial quotient*, one part of the final group count. Removing forty twice gets you to the same place as removing eighty once. Both paths have twenty groups and four items waiting, enough for one last group.

For 2,487 ÷ 6, read a row in two units. Removing 600 **items** credits 100 **groups** because six times one hundred is six hundred. The subtraction leaves 1,887 items. Crediting ten groups on that row would account for only sixty removed items. To test any stopping place, multiply the running group count by six and add the items left. The result must be the original dividend.

Suppose the pencil stops after the next removal of 1,800. You can verify 400 groups and 87 left since 2,400 + 87 = 2,487. **Resume with those 87.** Ten more groups use 60, leaving 27. Four more use 24, leaving 3. The *remainder* is what is left. Now it is too small to make another group. Check both conditions: 6 × 414 + 3 = 2,487 and 0 ≤ 3 < 6. If all four group counts are right but the final sum says 404, add 100 + 300 + 10 + 4 again. If the work simply stops at 87, the verified row is a place to continue.

## Read each place as an amount per share

In the [group ledger](../examples/quotient-zero-and-capacity.md#count-groups-of-four), three removals of 400 or one removal of 1,200 credit 300 groups of four. With five left there's one more group and one left over. Now [share the same 1,205 among four](../examples/quotient-zero-and-capacity.md#write-the-quotient-by-place). Twelve hundreds give each share three hundreds. There are zero hundreds left and the input has zero tens. Each share gets zero tens. Five ones give each share one and leave one. Read across the places: 301 R1. An answer of 31 R1 accounts for only 125, and 300 groups already fit. The two interpretations meet in the check 4 × 301 + 1 = 1,205.

## Resume at the zero tens

Open the [written division of 2,436 by six](../examples/grade-4/products-and-quotients.md#g48--which-zero-occupies-the-tens-place). Before writing, bound the quotient: 400 groups of six use 2,400 items and 410 groups would take 2,460. In the sharing calculation, trade two thousands for twenty hundreds. Together with the four hundreds, that's twenty-four hundreds shared six ways. Write four **hundreds** above. Zero **hundreds remain** below.

If your last checked line says **03 tens**, place a finger there. Three tens are available. Giving each of six shares even one ten would take six tens. Write **0 tens per share** above the 3 and subtract zero tens below. All three tens are still yours to exchange. Trade them for thirty ones, then include the waiting six ones. Share those thirty-six ones equally. Each share gets six ones and zero ones remain. For help, [rebuild the ten-for-one trade](06-bundles-and-trades.md#trade-and-return), then return here with the final input digit still waiting.

Ask a parent to point first at the zero left below the hundreds subtraction, then at the tens digit above. One answers “how much is left here?” and the other “how much does each share get here?” Read your scratch answer **406** into the answer field one place at a time. It fits the bound and 6 × (400 + 0 + 6) = 2,436. A copied 46 or 460 fails both checks. A visible claim of three tens per share would spend eighteen tens. With only a wrong final answer, you can test its value but cannot locate the step that produced it.

This written model uses up to four dividend digits and a positive one-digit divisor. It builds on [equal groups and their inverse product](09-equal-groups.md) and [exchange across columns](11-rounding-and-columns.md). As optional multiplication support, [recover each place in a product](18-partial-products.md). To [interpret the leftover rider](20-multistep-models.md), also identify the question and the unit of the requested answer.
