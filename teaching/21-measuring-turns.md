# Measuring a turn from its starting ray

**Why can one protractor tick have two numbers, and which one measures our turn?**

## Where does the turn begin?

In Grade 4, inspect the [line, ray, segment and trapezoid](../examples/grade-4/lines-and-turns.svg). A *point* marks a location. A *segment* ends at two points, a *ray* starts at one and continues in one direction, and a *line* continues in both directions. Draw each by choosing endpoints or adding continuation arrows. In the [trapezoid](../examples/grade-4/measuring-turns.md#g58--lines-endpoints-and-turns), extend AB and DC in both directions. These *parallel* lines stay the same distance apart. AD meets each at a square corner. Lines meeting this way are *perpendicular*.

Two rays from a shared endpoint, their *vertex*, form an angle. Choose the turn between them that you want to measure. In the [circle divided by the rays](../examples/grade-4/measuring-turns.md#degrees-on-a-circle), the arc, the curved piece of the circle between the rays, takes up a quarter of a full turn. A *degree* is one of 360 equal parts of a full turn. Counting one-degree intervals gives ninety for a quarter turn, a **right angle**. At B the interior turn is smaller, an **acute angle**. At C it exceeds 90° and stays below 180°, an **obtuse angle**. A larger circle makes a longer arc but the same fraction of a full turn.

## Choose zero before reading a number

Ask someone to follow the starting ray out to its printed zero on the [pair of protractors](../examples/grade-4/protractor-pair.svg). Place the center of the tool on the vertex. On the [right-starting construction](../examples/grade-4/measuring-turns.md#read-and-construct-on-a-protractor), count four ten-degree intervals from the rightward zero. You get 40°. The 140 at the same tick would measure from the opposite baseline. The dot halfway along the ray lines up with the very same tick. Even a shorter drawn arm still points in that direction.

Now construct the second angle. Draw a leftward ray, put the center over its endpoint, then count five tens and five single-degree intervals from the **left** zero. Mark that direction and draw a second ray from the vertex through the mark. The chosen turn is 55°. Reading 125° here would use the rightward zero. To check either construction, put zero along its sloping ray and measure back toward the original starting ray on the side containing the angle.

## Which intervals did you count twice?

*Adjacent angles* share a vertex and a side and have separate interiors. In the [two divided turns](../examples/grade-4/adjacent-turns.svg), the first 32° of the right angle leaves 58° because 32 + 58 = 90. In the 120° fan, 45° leaves 75°. Put the pieces back together to check the original directions. Now inspect the [camera pans](../examples/grade-4/measuring-turns.md#g59--which-degree-intervals-belong-to-each-part). From the start, 25° followed by 40° in the same direction spans 65°. Measuring 0° to 40° and 25° to 65° gives two readings of 40° each. From 25° to 40° the camera directions belong to **both** readings. Subtract that duplicated 15° from 80° to recover 65°.
