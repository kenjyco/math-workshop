# What makes a shape?

**Which features survive when a shape turns, or when two shapes join?**

## What can your finger trace?

Start anywhere on the [classroom shelf](../examples/kindergarten/shapes.svg). Look at the can beside the block. Trace its flat end on paper. The drawing is a circle. Pick up the can and turn it over. It has another circular end and a curved surface joining them. That's a cylinder, a *solid* that takes up space. A *face* is a flat surface of a solid. Feel how the cube rests on any of six square faces. The cone narrows to a tip and the sphere curves all the way around. Compare the [face and the whole object](../examples/kindergarten/shapes.md#the-face-and-the-whole) from a second direction.

A name can tell someone what to look for. A position word tells them where to look. “Above” needs a partner: the lid is above **the can**. Switch their names and the can is below the lid. Use the [shelf positions](../examples/kindergarten/shapes.md#where-is-it) to tell someone where to put the ball. To [name the flat shapes](../examples/kindergarten/shapes.md#which-shape-do-you-see), follow each edge. A *side* is a straight stretch of the outline, a *vertex* a corner where sides meet. Count six around the hexagon. [Turn the square](../examples/kindergarten/shapes.md#turn-it-or-make-it-smaller) until a corner points up. Its four equal sides still meet in square corners.

## Build an inside and an outside

In Kindergarten, [join the three sticks](../examples/kindergarten/building-shapes.md#build-a-roof-outline-and-draw-it) so that two stick ends meet at each of the three corners. Draw a dot at each joint and run a line along each stick. Can you travel all the way back to your first dot? Now [fit the two paper triangles together](../examples/kindergarten/building-shapes.md#fit-the-paper-pieces-together) along the entire sloping side AC. To *compose* means to make a shape from pieces. In the [roof and paper models](../examples/kindergarten/building-shapes.svg) the outside path A–B–C–D–A has four sides. The diagonal still tells you where to separate the pieces. Paper on both sides of it puts it inside the rectangle.

Grade 1 asks which features decide the name. In the [four outlines](../examples/grade-1/triangle-outlines.svg), trace each of the [possible triangles](../examples/grade-1/triangles-and-composites.md#building-and-drawing-a-triangle). In D the tip stops short. Your finger has to jump to get home. Shrinking or turning one of the closed triangles leaves that closing test true.

## Use the shape you just built

Hold a finger on the diagonal of the [square made of two triangles](../examples/grade-1/square-composition.svg). Invite someone to trace the boundary, the path around the outside. In the [second join](../examples/grade-1/triangles-and-composites.md#a-square-becomes-a-piece-of-a-rectangle), a whole square fits against that first square. Peel it off again. The diagonal still divides the remaining square into its original triangles.

Try that idea in depth with the [four cubes in pairs](../examples/grade-1/stacked-cubes.svg). In the [stacked box](../examples/grade-1/triangles-and-composites.md#a-pair-of-cubes-becomes-a-layer), one pair forms the bottom and another the top. Each full-face join hides two faces. Lift the top layer to see the faces that were touching. Four cubes with six faces each and four joins leave sixteen small faces exposed.

You can begin here by looking and tracing. To [test equal shares](13-shares-to-fractions.md), you'll also need to compare areas of pieces against a whole. To classify a turned figure, you'll use parallel lines and angle sizes as further tests.
