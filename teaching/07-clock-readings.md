# Clock readings and elapsed minutes

**What can the two hands tell us, and how long is it between readings?**

## Which hour has the short hand passed?

In Grade 1, cover the times under the [three clock faces](../examples/grade-1/clock-faces.svg). On the [first face](../examples/grade-1/clock-faces.md#reading-the-two-hands), the short hand points at 3 and the long hand points straight up. The hour is just beginning. Now follow the long hand halfway around to 6. One *hour* holds sixty minutes, so a half-hour holds thirty. While the long hand traveled, the short hand moved halfway from 3 to 4. Read **3:30**, half past three. Ask someone to cover the captions and point to the hour the short hand has already reached. To [draw the hands from a reading](../examples/grade-1/clock-faces.md#drawing-from-a-digital-time), try 12:30. The long hand points down, and the short one is already on its way to 1.

## What the station clock leaves out

Grade 2 reads and writes times in five-minute steps. On the [station dials](../examples/grade-2/station-clocks.svg) each numeral spacing takes five minutes. To [rebuild 7:20](../examples/grade-2/five-minute-clocks.md#twelve-five-minute-readings), take four such steps from 12 and move the short hand one third of the distance from 7 to 8. At five to eight the long hand has taken eleven steps and the short hand is *near* eight. It still has five minutes to go. Which train is it? The [two train departures](../examples/grade-2/five-minute-clocks.md#two-trains-at-five-to-eight) use identical hand positions. The morning event supplies **a.m.** and the evening event supplies **p.m.** The dial alone gives 7:55 for both.

## Cross the hour in two pieces

In Grade 3, each little tick on the [rehearsal clocks and timeline](../examples/grade-3/rehearsal-clocks.svg) is a minute mark. [Read the two hands](../examples/grade-3/minutes-across-four.md#reading-both-hands): three ticks past the 45-minute position gives 48, and the short hand is still before four. At the finish it's just past four and the minute hand is two ticks past five minutes. Those readings, 3:48 and 4:07 p.m., locate moments. *Elapsed time* counts the minutes between them.

Use [four o'clock as a stopping place](../examples/grade-3/minutes-across-four.md#crossing-four-oclock). From 48 minutes past to the next hour takes twelve minutes. Seven more reach 4:07, giving nineteen minutes altogether. Hide the beginning and travel backward nineteen: seven to 4:00, then twelve to 3:48. Hide the ending and reverse your trip. Counting time needs equal intervals; [ruler spaces](05-measuring-length.md) are a helpful model. A [full turn on a protractor](21-measuring-turns.md) also uses a starting direction and a scale in degrees. To [convert hours into minutes](16-measurement-units.md), you'll also use equal groups of sixty.
