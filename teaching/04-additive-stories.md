# Finding what a story asks for

**How can the same facts answer questions about different quantities?**

## Cover the quantity the story asks for

Use [parts and equality](03-parts-and-equality.md) to read the [Grade 1 tin stories](../examples/grade-1/stories-with-unknowns.md#buttons-going-into-or-coming-out-of-a-tin). Sit six buttons together and slide three more beside them. The *start* was six, the *change* was three arriving, the *result* is nine. Cover the arrivals. Count forward from six to nine to recover three. Cover the start instead. Lift away the three arrivals to recover six. Say the story with the number you found in place of the unknown. For a removal, run the movement backward: if three came out and six stayed, putting the three back must restore the original nine.

In the [tray stories](../examples/grade-1/stories-with-unknowns.md#two-parts-on-a-tray), the round and square buttons are two parts present **at the same time**. Cover either part of the seven. Setting the other part aside leaves exactly what was hidden. If only the whole four is known, slide one button at a time between its two named parts. The first part can hold zero through four. That's how you know when all splits have appeared.

A comparison asks how two amounts fit side by side. In the [two button rows](../examples/grade-1/stories-with-unknowns.md#two-collections-side-by-side), pair five of May's eight with Teo's five. Ask a parent to point only to the unpaired stretch. Those three give the *difference*. To recover May's row, add the stretch to five. To recover Teo's, take it off eight. Check by lining the rows up again. With [three pockets](../examples/grade-1/stories-with-unknowns.md#buttons-from-three-pockets), eight, five and seven are all parts. Take two of the five to fill the first ten and join the remaining three to seven. Both tens answer the question about **all three pockets**. Grade 1 stories and these three-addend totals stay within twenty.

## Carry a fact into a larger story

In Grade 2, the [mental fact grid](../examples/grade-2/fact-families-and-stories.md#facts-within-twenty) covers sums of two one-digit numbers. The cell where a row and column meet gives the two parts together. Cover either heading and subtract the other from the sum to recover it. Mental addition and subtraction extend through twenty. A larger story within one hundred can use several small facts.

Follow the [ticket tray](../examples/grade-2/fact-families-and-stories.md#a-tray-of-tickets). Six of nine tickets take 24 up to 30. The last three take it to 33. If the story gives both endpoints, the change is those six plus three. Try a missing start, a missing part and a missing comparison row. Replace the unknown in each original relationship: adding nine to 24 restores 33, removing nine from 33 restores 24, and matching 24 against 33 leaves nine.

Now inspect the [two windows](../examples/grade-2/fact-families-and-stories.md#two-steps-at-the-ticket-window). A *subtotal* answers a question about part of the story. The first window's 33 tickets answer “how many there?” Point to the second window's forty and match 33 of them. Seven are unmatched. The requested answer is **seven more tickets**. Work back from forty by seven, then by nine, to find the morning's 24. If the work stops at 33, keep that subtotal and return to the comparison. Later, checking the shipment question uses this same habit with multiplication, division and units.
