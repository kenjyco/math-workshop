# Rounding and keeping track of every exchange

**Which moves preserve a number, and which change its value?**

## Find the neighbors first

Use [intervals](05-measuring-length.md) to picture the [Grade 3 rounding lines](../examples/grade-3/rounding-and-exchanges.md#both-sides-of-a-midpoint). To round to tens or hundreds, find the neighboring multiples of that unit. The *midpoint* sits equally far from both. Ninety already sits on a ten. From 94 the shorter walk is to 90. From 96 it is to 100. At 95 both walks take five steps. For a halfway whole number here choose the greater multiple. On the hundreds line, 249 and 251 sit on opposite sides of the 250 tie.

In the [two deliveries](../examples/grade-3/rounding-and-exchanges.md#two-ways-to-count-a-delivery), rounding gives 370 + 260, an **estimate** of 630 envelopes. Count exactly by combining like places: 500 + 110 + 15 = 625. Or move two, then thirty, from the second pile into the first. You can now count 400 and 225. That move used the same envelopes. Rounding the two original piles up had added two and three, which explains the five-envelope difference. To [subtract 178 from 602](../examples/grade-3/rounding-and-exchanges.md#taking-envelopes-from-a-full-carton), take out 180, then put back the two you took in excess. Check 424 + 178 = 602. These Grade 3 calculations stay within 1,000.

## A digit takes its value from its place

Grade 4 extends the [place table](../examples/grade-4/place-and-exchanges.md#g41--reading-the-places) through one million. In 770, the left seven counts hundreds and the right seven counts tens. One hundred contains ten tens, so the left seven contributes ten times the value of its neighbor. Read 704,009 as seven hundred four thousand nine. Its parts are 700,000 + 4,000 + 9. Move the written 9 from ones to tens, leaving zero ones. Now the numeral is **704,090**. Its last contribution grew from 9 to 90, increasing the number by **81**. Compare from the left until you reach those differing tens digits. Their values decide the sign: 704,009 < 704,090. By contrast, opening one ten into ten ones gives you exactly the same ten objects back. [Try the material trade](06-bundles-and-trades.md#trade-and-return) if you want to feel the difference.

For [rounding to a selected place](../examples/grade-4/place-and-exchanges.md#nearest-multiple), check that the distances to the neighbors add to one place unit. From 999,600 the two distances are 600 and 400 when rounding to thousands. One more thousand after 999,000 is 1,000,000, the nearer end. An exact multiple stays where it is.

## Follow the unit through the zeros

The Grade 4 *standard algorithm* for addition and subtraction lines up equal place units. Trace the [two written calculations](../examples/grade-4/place-and-exchanges.md#g42--following-each-exchange). In 59,986 + 78, fourteen ones send one ten left and keep four ones. Sixteen tens send one hundred left and keep six tens. Ten hundreds send one thousand and leave zero hundreds. In the thousands column, nine thousands plus the incoming thousand make ten thousands. Exchange them for one unit in the ten-thousands column and leave zero thousands. The five ten-thousands receive that unit and become six. Check by adding 14 to reach 60,000 and then the remaining 64.

For 50,002 − 487 ask a parent to point to each receiving column and ask where its ten units came from. Open a ten-thousand. Pass one of its ten thousands on as ten hundreds. Pass one of those on as ten tens, and one of those as ten ones. The middle columns each keep nine. Pause at 40,000 + 9,000 + 900 + 90 + 12 and check that it still names 50,002. Now take seven ones, eight tens and four hundreds. Adding the removed amounts back to 49,515 gives 49,915, then 49,995, then 50,002. To [bound a division answer](19-quotient-places.md), also use multiplication. For [capacity estimates](20-multistep-models.md), also identify what the remainder counts.
