# Math Workshop V2 — North Star

**Build a complete, accurate K–4 mathematics foundation that supports an activity-first workshop: start where you are, explore in any direction, understand why the mathematics works, and return to your work without friction.**

## A verified foundation before the teaching interface

Begin with the mathematical concepts, definitions, terminology, worked examples, and visual representations needed across kindergarten through fourth grade. Connect standards to concepts without treating the standards list as the only possible teaching sequence.

The first implementation pass is examples and concise explanations of those examples. After their correctness and coverage are established, a separate teaching pass creates thoughtfully arranged explanations. Interactive activities then apply and combine that foundation. A glossary alone, a premature lesson book, or a collection of unrelated mini-games would leave the foundation incomplete.

## Activity-first experience

Children primarily enter through activities suited to what they want to explore or practice. As the collection grows, make it visually organized and easy to sift through, with meaningful groupings, recognizable illustrations or symbols, and clear text labels. Avoid a wall of undifferentiated choices. Choose discovery features for their usefulness, not to build a catalog framework.

Learners can start with current work, revisit earlier concepts, try a related idea, or peek ahead. Grade labels and suggested sequences provide context; they do not lock content behind completed activities. Support understandable touch/keyboard interaction, readable layouts, and access that does not depend on interpreting an icon or color alone.

## Help that can go backward, forward, and sideways

An activity's **Help me understand** view explains the relevant concept using clear examples. The explanation stands on its own, with optional connections at its end:

- **This builds on…** links to foundational ideas, each with its own explanation.
- **What can I learn next?** links to ideas the current concept helps support.
- Related concepts can provide useful sideways connections.

An advanced concept may draw on several foundations. Its help view should show those other foundations too; arriving from one concept does not mean the learner has mastered all of them. The learner can stop after one explanation or continue as deeply as useful. Preserve a clear route back and the exact unfinished activity state.

Use shared explanations rather than duplicating place value, grouping, or another foundation for every activity. The content has relationships; the interface need not display a complex graph. Prerequisites describe mathematics and possible support, not required completion checklists.

Future topics must be clearly distinguished from explanations already available. Do not offer a completed-looking help link to unwritten material.

## Parent bridges belong with the examples

Parents should be able to connect unfamiliar terminology or representations to methods they recognize. Add a concise bridge after a suitable concept, example, or related group, explaining how the mathematical views agree. Different parents learned different approaches; avoid assuming one universal traditional method.

For multiplication, for example, expanded partial products, an area representation, and a familiar vertical calculation can express the same place-value reasoning. The bridge should make that connection visible. It is still foundational example content, not a full lesson or a separate parent course postponed until later.

Later teaching can reuse the same material in child help and parent support without crowding the activity. Printable references are a useful later presentation of verified content where appropriate.

## Complete coverage and selective activity priority

The foundational map covers all K–4 domains. Later interactive work first emphasizes multiplication and division, including place value, partial products, factors, multi-step completion, and checking work. Counting, grouping, and early arithmetic remain part of the complete foundation, with their interactive activities coming later.

Support sufficient working space, clear place-value structure, and careful completion. Avoid treating every execution slip as a lack of conceptual understanding. Activities may combine several concepts; every standard does not need a separate game.

## Expansion without an oversized first release

Plan K–4 in depth and keep later grades at roadmap level. Concepts may develop across grades, with appropriately scoped examples added over time. Adding grade five should be another bounded extension, not a rebuild of the project or a requirement to implement high-school mathematics now.

Go, SQLite, and Svelte are the provisional application direction. A small equivalent Elm/Svelte interaction may inform the final frontend choice before interactive implementation. It should not create two production applications, a plugin system, or a graph database.

The later interface should follow the actual Kenjyco Visual Identity Kit v2.1 when it has been inspected. Preserve an inviting, clear, coherent visual identity without inventing unseen design details or making private source assets a runtime dependency.

## Success scenario

A learner opens a multiplication activity, asks why a step works, explores place value or another foundation, peeks at a related future idea, and returns to the unfinished problem with all work intact. A parent can understand the same example through familiar methods. Every step rests on accurate mathematics and the learner remains free to choose where to go.
